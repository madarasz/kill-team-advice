# Canoptek Circle - Advice

## TL;DR

1. **Canoptek Circle is a patient counter-punch team: "a spider, not a tiger."** Hang the Tomb Crawlers, Geomancer, Macrocyte Reanimator and Macrocyte Accelerator back. Chip and score with Macrocyte Warriors. Punish whatever commits with Crawler guns. Stay outside threat ranges and keep the last 1–2 activations. Most losses come from getting hungry for damage. **[strong consensus]**
2. **Retrieval is the default TacOp ("when in doubt, Retrieval").** It gives a safe 4+ VP:
   - Home falls to a support piece.
   - Mid goes late, to a Crawler.
   - The enemy home falls to a 3-APL breached Warrior (move, stun, retrieve).
   Plant Banner is the main alternative. **[strong consensus]**
3. **Default kit: Krak Grenades + Nanoscarab Caskets + Awakened Obelisk Nodes + one terrain flex slot** (barricades or razor wire). Krak Grenades are essentially mandatory: they are the Warriors' only real threat. **[strong consensus]**
4. **Treat the 18-wound Tomb Crawlers as precious, not as face-tanks.** Always bring the Transdimensional Isolator. Obscure the 50mm base, do the math on what can kill it, and don't dangle it into melee. **[strong consensus]**
5. **Lay the matrix as a line, not a triangle, across home and mid**, stretched so your targeting lines cross it. Fight *on* the matrix, since the +1 APL (max 3) and Accurate 1 vanish the moment you step off. Learn a set layout for every map. **[strong consensus]**
6. **Always budget CP for Transdynamic Amplification, Shield Flare and Sacrificial Thrall**, and keep the Geomancer within 3" of a Warrior. The team is CP-hungry, so plan on only 1–2 Command Re-rolls a game. **[strong consensus]**

## Operative Selection

**Standard roster (near-universal):**
- Geomancer, Macrocyte Accelerator and Macrocyte Reanimator.
- 2 Tomb Crawlers: one with the Transdimensional Isolator, one with twin gauss.
- 3 Macrocyte Warriors, with guns swapped per game.

Only one Isolator is allowed, and double-gauss is "never correct". **[strong consensus]**

**Tomb Crawler (Transdimensional Isolator)** — always take it.
- Dimensional Banishment gets around medics, Medic!, Fellgor Ravager Frenzy and "not incapacitated" leader abilities.
- It stops Hierotek Circle placing Reanimation markers, but only when the banishment does the kill. Aim a hit or two at a model, not overkill damage.
- It is the piece opponents focus above everything. Before leaving the drop zone, ask whether one shot is worth losing it.
- It does nothing against NEMESIS operatives or the Red Terror NPO.

**[strong consensus]**

**Tomb Crawler (general play)** — 18 wounds, 3+ save, 50mm base.
- **Keep it back:** "if you think you need to move a Crawler or Geo, you probably don't."
- Stand behind the matrix and step onto it only for aggressive plays.
- The big base makes obscuring easy: stand within 1" of heavy terrain and draw every line.
- It is no longer a face-tank. Two severe chainsword fights, or four melta crits, kill it even with Shield Flare and Hypershielding up. Respect melee-heavy teams.
- **Dominate:** it is worth 2 tokens to an enemy playing Dominate.
- **Home anchor:** on your home objective it counts as 3 APL for control. With Hypershielding, Shield Flare and the Macrocyte Reanimator nearby, it is very hard to shift.

**[strong consensus]**

**Geomancer** — the engine; never trade it.

Its turn is "move, command, breach, mission action. Repeat."
- **Timing is the key skill.** Don't burn it early (e.g. looting mid in the first activation of TP2). From TP2 on, every Geomancer action should change the board. **[strong consensus]**
- **Molecular Breach lasts until the operative next moves.**
  - Pre-stage it on Crawlers or Warriors as early as TP1 and leave them as standing threats; opponents will avoid the zone. You can stack it on 2–3 models over time, but can breach only one operative per activation.
  - It measures horizontal distance only, so it lands models on vantage or upper floors like Fly, and it goes through walls.
  - It can target the Geomancer itself, but that teleport replaces its later move, so do it last.
- **Canoptek Control** (command) can't be used while counteracting, and the commanded operative can't move more than 2" during that action.
  - Use it to put a Crawler on Guard before the Crawler activates.
  - Use it to make an expended operative (already on Engage) Shoot or Guard again.
  - Use it to shut a hatch behind a shot, or to make the Macrocyte Accelerator Overcharge a second time.
  - A 2" command move plus the target's own move, dash and shoot sets up long Warrior and Crawler threats.
- **Geomantic Disturbance ("throwing rocks")** is a finisher.
  - It hits everything within 2" of a terrain point the Geomancer can see, and auto-kills 1W models.
  - Use it on your own low-wound Warrior that an enemy is hugging ("a classic").
  - The killzone floor isn't a valid point.
  - Its Quake profile is 5 attacks at 1/2 damage; use it to chip and stun, not to delete.
- **Protection:** keep it obscured on the matrix line, within 3" of a Warrior for Sacrificial Thrall. Its survivability (Macrocyte Reanimator, heal, Shield Flare) is decent, but losing it early is crippling.

**Macrocyte Warrior** — free, expendable scorers and harassers.
- They give no VP when killed. Expendable Construct covers kill-based TacOps and scoring, and Warriors don't count for Martyrs or Envoy.
- **Trades:** their stat line (weak melee, Aggressive Defence dealing a flat 1 damage on a 2+) means they lose most trades. Spend their APL on mission actions, chip and body-blocking, not fights.
- **Attrition:** expose at most one Warrior per TP (except TP4). Losing two costs activations, because only one comes back per TP. **[strong consensus]**
- **The deep Warrior:** +1 APL from the Macrocyte Accelerator plus Molecular Breach sends one 3-APL Warrior onto the enemy home to retrieve, krak or deny. It eats 1–2 enemy activations to answer, and it banks VP even if it dies. **[strong consensus]**
- **TP1 threat:** a Geomancer command (2") plus a +1 APL Warrior's move, dash and shoot threatens about 14" from the drop zone. That limits where elites stage.
- **Guns** push-fit, so swap them per game. A reanimated Warrior can take a different weapon.
  - Lean **blast (Tesla)**. Take all blast into hordes and on close boards: "blast and fishing for crits".
  - Keep at least one blast Warrior in every list to "keep them honest".
  - **Piercing (gauss scalpel)** is still fine into elites, or bring Warriors back with it once the Krak Grenades are spent.

**[strong consensus]**

**Macrocyte Reanimator** — the most important support piece.
- **Positioning:** keep it behind the lines and off Engage, within 6" of the Crawlers and/or on the matrix. Don't group it with them where one blast hits both. Its 6" usually reaches without the matrix sitting at the back.
- **Reanimate:**
  - The free Dash after reanimating must end within its control range or inside the matrix.
  - Both operatives take −1 APL until the end of their next activations. Offset it with the Macrocyte Accelerator.
  - Nanoscarab Beam is once per turning point.
- **Limits:**
  - It can't bring back an operative killed while in control range (including hatchway fights).
  - It can't reanimate itself, and can't reanimate while it is the target of a blast or torrent.
  - A reanimated Warrior can be set up inside an enemy's control range if that enemy is in your drop zone.
- **Late game:** it is a real combat piece (more melee attacks than a Warrior). TP4 dash-shoot-move with it and the Accelerator is rewarding.

**[strong consensus]**

**Macrocyte Accelerator** — the action monkey.
- **+1 APL buff:** it persists off the matrix. Buff the operative that must act off the matrix: the deep Warrior, a runner, or the Macrocyte Reanimator (to offset reanimate −1 APL or a medic penalty).
- **Stuns:** the buff cancels a stun.
- **Geomancer:** it can never buff the Geomancer, because the Geomancer is a Cryptek.
- **Setup:** buff an expended Warrior in TP1 and breach it, then teleport, stun and retrieve in TP2.
- **Cranial Overload (−1 APL on an enemy) is niche.** Use it through the matrix from safety on an overextended enemy, or as a TP4 last-activation retake.
- **Bait:** it makes decent bait on a forward vantage in some matchups.

## TacOp Selection

**Retrieval** — the default pick, taken in the large majority of games (one top player's season: 44-1-1 with it almost every game). **[strong consensus]**
- Safe 4+ VP: home via the Macrocyte Accelerator or Macrocyte Reanimator.
- Mid late via a Crawler (breach, shoot, retrieve). A Crawler holding the token on the matrix is hard to force to drop it.
- The enemy home falls to a 3-APL breached Warrior with a stun grenade: move, stun, retrieve for an "easy 5".
- No rush: don't spend 3 APL retrieving everything in TP2.
- The Geomancer **cannot** retrieve or pick up markers through nodes. A real operative must reach each objective.
- **Avoid it when the crit op is Energy Cells.** The two anti-synergise, since you can't carry two markers. Take Plant Banner instead.

**Plant Banner** — strong alternative, often scoring 5–6+ once secured.
- It forces the opponent to come into your matrix buffs. A node can control a planted banner but cannot plant one.
- Best on maps with an unassailable vantage or an isolated corner: park the matrix, a node and a Crawler on it, and body-block 32mm bases off it.
- A breaching Crawler can plant in the last activation.
- **Never take it when out-activated.** The opponent saves their last activation to deny you.
- **Never take it** into Kroot (Farstalker Kinband), Ratlings, or melee teams that can kill a Crawler in one activation and have a hard-to-charge spot to use.

**Scout Enemy Movement** — situational. It works only when the opponent doesn't expect it and the map is high-visibility. The Geomancer can command a double scout while the support pieces sit idle anyway. After TP2 most of your operatives are on Engage, so it is hard to score late. The community mostly leans against it.

**Flank** — **[heavily debated]**. Some players rate it on Tomb World maps where the opponent must commit to cross ("flank tek": teleport a Warrior late in TP2, breach another to the opposite flank). Others call it dreadful there and a way to feed kills. It requires operatives wholly within.

**Envoy / Martyrs** — don't take them. Warriors can't be the Envoy or count as Martyrs, so only your key pieces could score: "a recipe for disaster".

**Enemy TacOps:** kill-based ops (Seek & Destroy, Steal Intelligence) are poor into you, because expendable Warriors give nothing. The exception is Dominate: a Crawler is worth 2 tokens to them.

## Equipment Selection

**Krak Grenades** — essentially mandatory, often two.
- They are the Warriors' only real threat, and they force the opponent to respect all three Warriors.
- They get Ceaseless from Transdynamic Amplification, but not the matrix's Accurate.
- Punish elites sitting on mid with a Krak plus a Geomancer command shoot.

**[strong consensus]**

**Nanoscarab Caskets** — core pick in most lists. Once per turning point, however it's triggered. It's the difference between a Warrior being injured or not. It's more valuable into midrange and hordes than into elites, where some drop it for terrain or an Ammo Cache.

**Awakened Obelisk Nodes** — near-auto.
- Its main value is the skipped activation, which secures last activation when activation counts are close (Wrecka Krew, Orb, Banner fights).
- It also allows aggressive node pushes and pull-backs.
- It loses value into 10–11-activation teams, and can hand elites a counteract. Leave it home when it won't actually buy last activation.

**[strong consensus]**

**Terrain flex (barricades, razor wire, mines)** — "hard to go wrong with". Build a "fort" or "wall of terrain":
- **Staging:** use a Heavy Barricade as a staging spot for a Crawler or the Geomancer, or as a safe medic spot at the back of the matrix. Crawlers ignore light cover for valid-target purposes, so the Heavy Barricade matters more than lights for them.
- **Razor wire on a centre objective with no map cover** stops the opponent dropping light cover there, so whoever stands on it gets shot. Stack it with Cryptogravitic Repulsion for −2" of charge.
- **Stack cover:** a portable barricade plus a wall gives In Cover *and* Obscured.
- **Mines:** a mine on a vantage or teleporter deters charges and landings. Mind your own operatives.

**[strong consensus]**

**Stun Grenades** — situational.
- Uses: teleport-stun-retrieve (about 66% success), denying banners and icons, and hitting 2-APL operatives or Mandrake teleports.
- It is a 3+ single-dice gamble that costs a flex slot.

**Ammo Cache** — useful when you don't need terrain. Drop it at Crawler staging spots or on the TP1 Warrior's route to fix Isolator reliability. It **cannot** be used from anywhere through a node (Obelisk Node Control only covers objective markers).

**Ladders** — near-mandatory on some open maps to get Crawlers and support onto vantage.

**Smoke Grenades** — low value. Crawlers are tanky enough with normal obscuring. They do fit a centred 50mm base.

**Comms Device** — unnecessary. The Geomancer's support abilities reach anything on the matrix, so keep operatives within 6" of it or on the matrix.

**Phase Shifter** — rarely worth it. Enemy piercing comes in volume, and the Geomancer shouldn't be getting shot. Take a Heavy Barricade instead.

**Matrix Manipulator** — skip ("pretty useless"). It only extends the matrix for one activation. **[strong consensus]**

## Faction Rule Advice

**Obelisk Node Matrix** — inside it, operatives get +1 APL (max 3) and Accurate 1. Many ploys only need the matrix *intervening*.

- **Lay a line, not a triangle.** A triangle's interior isn't on the matrix; it only adds a third line. Form one only when it costs nothing on objective control. **[strong consensus]**
- **Covering two objectives:**
  - Touch **home and mid** (or mid and the enemy objective), and stretch the line across as much of the board as you can so that flanking enemies are still shot through it.
  - Keep it close enough that Warriors can move and dash onto it.
  - On open boards it is usually a line between two objectives; on close boards an L reaching toward two.
  - **[strong consensus]**
- **Keep the matrix in front of you.** You should never have to move past it to shoot; fights should happen *on* it. Don't set it deep in your drop zone.
- **The APL is a limit, not a resource.** The +1 (max 3) applies only while standing on the matrix and disappears the moment you step off.
  - Start an activation on the matrix, take two actions there, and leave with the third.
  - You can't shoot on it, then reposition and dash off at 3 APL.
  - Only the Macrocyte Accelerator's buff persists off the matrix.
  - **[strong consensus]**
- **Touching vs intervening.**
  - APL and Accurate need you to touch the matrix.
  - For Transdynamic Amplification, draw targeting lines from the part of your base on or behind the matrix. You pick the point and must use it consistently.
  - Enemies choose their own lines, so they can dodge Hypershielding by shooting around the matrix.
- **Infinite height.** The matrix extends vertically, so vantage above a floor-level line counts as on it. Don't put nodes on vantage over a floor-level objective: control needs the node within 1".
- **Obelisk Node Control:**
  - Nodes control uncontested objectives with no operative. Any enemy contesting turns it off, regardless of APL, so the Geomancer's node actions are "camera shy". **[strong consensus]**
  - Controlling needs no visibility; "within 1"" works through walls.
  - Through a node the Geomancer can do mission actions (loot, secure, download) and Operate Hatch.
  - It **cannot** Retrieve, Pick Up Marker (so no Energy Cells or retrieval tokens), or Plant Banner, though a node can control an already-planted banner. It can't use equipment through a node either.
  - A lone node outside the matrix still controls objectives and opens hatches. Only Accurate, APL and the ploys need a connected matrix.
  - Starting TP2 with a node on their home objective while a Warrior threatens it forces the opponent to step out and contest.
- **Nodes control but don't contest.** Rulings on this vary (e.g. against Raveners' Plant Devices), so ask your TO.
- **Support over the matrix.** The Macrocyte Reanimator and Macrocyte Accelerator can heal, reanimate and buff anyone on the matrix without line of sight. Don't string the matrix back just to reach them, though; 6" is usually plenty.
- **Placement and moves:**
  - Nodes start wholly within your half and push forward with Animate Obelisk Nodes.
  - The strategic-gambit move shifts each node up to 3" horizontally. TTS tools over-allow it, so measure manually.
- **Static vs mobile.**
  - Most set the matrix once and barely move it; that is about 85% of the value, and set layouts per map are close to "solved".
  - Some push nodes up in TP1 to give Warriors 3 APL and early Shield Flare against hordes, then pull back against melee.
  - Pre-plan a default layout for every map at an event.

## Ploys

- **CP budget.** The team is CP-hungry:
  - Reserve CP for Shield Flare in TP2–3 and for Sacrificial Thrall.
  - Spend the rest on Strategy ploys, then Command Re-rolls.
  - Going second earns CP. Pay for Transdynamic Amplification in TP2 and switch on the defensive ploys in TP3–4 once fighting starts.

### Strategic Ploys

- **Transdynamic Amplification** — "don't leave home without it". Ceaseless whenever your targeting line crosses the matrix, including Warrior Krak Grenades. Fire it in TP1 against aggressive teams. **[strong consensus]**
- **Souldrain** — −1 melee damage for the rest of that enemy activation, against operatives that aren't on the matrix, so order your fights accordingly. The preferred CP spend against melee (Blades of Khaine, Wolf Scouts, Chaos Cult, Murderwing, Exodites, Nemesis Claw). It doesn't trigger on enemies that fly or teleport out. **[strong consensus]**
- **Cryptogravitic Repulsion** — use it only to stop a top-of-turn charge. Premeasure threats so you rarely need it. It cuts 1" (2" with razor wire). It does not affect boost or fly landings.
- **Hypershielding** — the defensive buff for operatives in the matrix. Great on a Crawler holding home. It isn't enough on its own against focused plasma or melta at 18 wounds, and shooters can draw lines around the matrix.

### Firefight Ploys

- **Shield Flare** — keep CP for it every TP. On a lone objective Warrior it makes the opponent waste 2–3 activations. On a Crawler it keeps it alive through an obscured heavy shot. **[strong consensus]**
- **Sacrificial Thrall** — always keep the Geomancer within 3" of a Warrior. X" Devastating doesn't switch it off (only blast and torrent do), so it is great into teams without blast. The redirected operative counts as in control range (so it can't be reanimated) but gets combat support. **[strong consensus]**
- **Animate Obelisk Nodes** — it counts as an activation. Use it to skip and secure last activation (equal-activation teams, Wrecka Krew, Orb, Banner fights), or slam a node onto an objective and then activate the Geomancer to tap it. Node movement is horizontal and can't pass Tomb World walls. Into elites it can hand them a counteract. **[strong consensus]**
- **Nodal Response** — near-useless. Its only real use is swapping Hypershielding for Souldrain. **[strong consensus]**

## Terrain

### Volkus / Octarius (open)

**Nodes and matrix**
- Walls aren't Wall terrain here. Nodes measure and move freely through ruins and strongholds (only the killzone edge blocks them), and the matrix reaches upper floors.

**Crawler lines and staging**
- Crawlers see from their low faces, so they can't see models on top of strongholds. Hang the head around wall corners or use the ruins' 1" doorway, and plan it before moving up.
- Crawlers ignore light cover and are almost always valid targets. A Heavy Barricade, strongholds and breaching from them matter. Some maps force a 2" climb tax on 50mm bases.
- Ladders or TP1 breaches get Crawlers and support onto vantage.
- A breached Crawler on the floor threatens to fly up without committing.
- Keep support models on the ground floor and shooters up, or the reverse.

**Map and board-specific plays**
- Crawlers can't go on Guard here, but can on close boards.
- Plant Banner is strong when you can base-block a vantage (e.g. Volkus 6, Stronghold A).
- Beware TP1 seek-light stuns and long vantage lanes.
- Against Raveners, Blades of Khaine and Exodites, stage big bases on vantage with ladders.
- Against Kasrkin, stage aggressive Warriors.
- Plague Marines, Kasrkin and Wolf Scouts are easier here because you out-activate them.
- Murderwing and Vespid Stingwings are hardest here.
- On Volkus 6, take the side that lets you bunker in the big stronghold.

### Tomb World / Gallowdark (close quarters)

**Nodes and matrix**
- The matrix must be a straight, clear line. Any Wall-terrain clip (including a door frame's sides) breaks it, so it only links through open hatchways.
- Nodes can't move through walls, only doors, and can get stuck in traffic.
- Plan on 2 nodes forming the matrix. Park the third by a hatch so the Geomancer can open it through the node, completing the matrix through the doorway; otherwise use it alone for objective control.
- Layouts are close to solved per map:
  - TW2: line down the hallway.
  - TW3: no node on your home objective.
  - TW5: last node on the enemy home, razor wire in the hallway.
  - Keep the lines connected early on maps with distant rooms, and creep forward with the gambit.

**Guarding and doorways**
- Guard plus obscured is the key tool. Crawlers can Guard here.
- Stand a Crawler to the side of a hatchway at max 1", draw through the door and Guard: obscured while shooting unobscured.
- A Geomancer command can put a Crawler on Guard after it shoots.
- Keep the Macrocyte Reanimator at the door looking into a room, not inside it.

**Breaching and TP1**
- Molecular Breach goes through walls. Stage a breached Crawler behind a wall until the enemy moves in, or breach a Warrior through two walls onto the enemy objective.
- In TP1, put the Geomancer on Engage while obscured for a double breach ("basically free").

**Board-specific plays**
- Take all blast Warriors plus Krak Grenades.
- Lights and razor wire in small corridors can make Crawlers uncharge-able against full-melee teams.
- Geomantic Disturbance only reaches 2" around an open door.
- Drop Awakened Obelisk Nodes for terrain equipment when you don't need the skip.

**Matchups on close boards**
- Favoured into Celestian Insidiants, Xv26 Stealth Battlesuits, Kasrkin, Goremongers and Blades of Khaine (Guard in a room, throw rocks).
- Harder into Raveners (the tunnel ignores walls) and Plague Marines (smoke plus poison Guard).
- Against Stealth Battlesuits, breach the matrix into the room holding their home at the start of TP2, then teleport Warriors through the walls onto it.

## Generic Advice

- **Play the waiting game.**
  - The opponent must eventually engage to score, and then loses the gunfight.
  - Hang back outside threat ranges, chip with Warriors doing crit and TacOp, punish over-extension with Crawler guns, and push late or on a clear opening.
  - "Never leave the matrix for absolutely no reason."
  - **[strong consensus]**
- **Premeasure every charge threat.** It is "a huge part of this team". Know exactly what can efficiently kill a Crawler, the Geomancer or a support piece. Don't clump against blast.
- **Always keep the last 1–2 activations**, via Warriors held back, the Awakened Obelisk Nodes skip, or going second.
- **Draw every targeting line on the table.** It builds intuition and shows the opponent where they must go.
- **Set a short goal before each TP.** For example: a Crawler guarding a corridor, a Warrior on the objective on Guard by TP2, the Geomancer breaching then guarding.
- **Stage a mid loot with a 3-APL Warrior on the matrix.** If the enemy puts 3 APL there, remove them with a Crawler. An expended Warrior on mid invites a charge-hug from melee teams.
- **Pick sides by scoring upside, not only defence.**
- **Explain the team to opponents** in this order: operatives, then matrix, then Geomancer last. Warn them before each effect triggers.
- **The team is not intuitive**, with "no solved answer"; expect around 30 games to click. Get reps on both open and close boards, since the team plays very differently on each, and practise your worst matchups and map setups.
