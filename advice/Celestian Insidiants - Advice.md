# Celestian Insidiants - Advice

## TL;DR
- **You are a tanky, counter-punching control team, not a killing team.** Build a castle on home + centre, make the enemy over-commit to get kills, then trade back harder as your survivors ramp. Play the mission and out-activate — you win on points, not corpses. [strong consensus]
- **Get operatives inspiring fast and wide in TP1–2, and never let an uninspired sister be charged or shot.** Inspiring powers damage reduction, longer charges, the death-benediction, and half your ploys. Killing an uninspired sister is the opponent's big win. [strong consensus]
- **Standard one box: all specialists + Superior with the crossbow + fill the last 4 slots with Warriors and Cremators** (2 Cremators / 2 Warriors is the flexible default; magnetise the arms and the Superior's gun). Take at least one Cremator almost always. [strong consensus]
- **Saintly Relics and Psyk-out Grenades are near-auto equipment.** Auto-flagellator is a trap that breaks your own breakpoints. [strong consensus]
- **Holy Resilience (damage reduction) is your defining ploy — play it most rounds after TP1;** bank CP for the firefight ploys (strike-on-death, +1" charge, ceaseless), which are stronger than most strategy ploys. [strong consensus]
- **Plant Banner, Dominate and Envoy are your reliable TacOps; you lean Crit-primary because the team's TacOp set is weak.** [strong consensus]
- **The team's structural weakness is fast, shooty teams that kite you and terrain that blocks your charges;** you struggle to reach them and can't reduce shooting.

## Operative Selection
*(Roster is fixed: Superior + your specialists + the last 4 slots filled with Warriors/Cremators. Order below is how hard each earns its place.)*

- **Insidiant Abjuror — near-undroppable.** Top statcheck piece and the anchor of your castle. Her "Get Down Madam President" support lets the protected model fight back at +1 (she hits on 2s) even with no other enemies in control range — but it's once per turning point, and does **not** work against Blast or Torrent weapons. An inspired Abjuror with damage reduction is a nightmare to shift. Get her inspired early — she castles rather than charges, so her inspiration is easy to forget. [strong consensus]
- **Insidiant Censor — near-undroppable, pairs with the Abjuror.** The null-field aura (enemies at −1 to hit, movement denial) plus a brutal bonkstaff, and she counts as 3 on objectives. Park her within ~2" of the Abjuror behind cover: anyone shooting her within 2" eats the Abjuror's deterrent, anyone charging fights at −1 into the Abjuror's combat support ("I'm on 2s, you're on 4s"). She hits on 4s herself, so she wants ceaseless/combat support to kill, not to solo a frontline. Drop her only into dice-fixing shooters (Brood Brothers, Wreckas) or teams that already hit you on 3s. [strong consensus]
- **Insidiant Superior — mandatory. Take the crossbow (stakethrower) ~90–95% of games.** Silent, full range, Dev 2 / Piercing 1 / Lethal 5+ when inspiring — she plinks and spreads inspiration from safety ("crossbow Batman"). Her crossbow is genuinely good (3 APL, unlike the basic Warrior bows). Keep her back-but-not-on-home to threaten silent shots; she's worse in melee than a Warrior and you need her alive as an inspiration engine. The inferno/melta pistol (3" range) is a trap — take it only to breach a Tomb World wall for 1 AP, and **always** into Stealth Suits (nothing else in their team is shootable). [strong consensus on the crossbow]
- **Insidiant Reliquarius — inspiration battery and home-objective holder.** Hands out inspiring for no AP and only needs to be *seen by* the target, not to see it — so she can move 9", shoot silently and inspire in one activation ("a discount Superior"; many pilots played this too passively for a long time). She's the first cut on maps where she can't reach or see out of your home objective TP1. Her melee is deliberately weak.
- **Insidiant Denuncia — powerful but heavily debated.** She is your only pre-death access to the faction rule (a benediction without anyone dying), one of the best medics in the game (D3+2 heal at 6", enough to un-injure a 9-wound sister), an on-demand +1 APL to widen a threat range TP1, and a free Suspect & Eliminate. The counter-camp drops her for an extra Warrior — her own weapons rarely kill a 7-wound body, and if you have no clear job for her the slot does more as a Warrior. Take her when you have a role in mind for her (healbot, tempo, guaranteed stun); leave her home into aggressive Space Marines or when you can't keep her inspired. [heavily debated]
- **Insidiant Mortisanctus — a scalpel and a "distraction carnifex".** Pre-parry + Brutal + Lethal 5+ make her a near-unkillable melee threat that diverts enemy traffic and solos shield/parry power-pieces (marines, shield guys). Stage her a real ~10" of movement from a target 8" away so the +1" charge ploy threatens asymmetric charges. Some pilots drop her into teams of ≤9-wound bodies (a Warrior 2-taps them and keeps charge-fight-shoot flexibility) — reasonable, but most keep her for the threat projection.
- **Insidiant Cremators — take at least one almost always; take two into hordes, 5+ saves / low-wound bodies, and close boards.** Their job is spreading inspiring and reliable chip, not raw damage — they self-inspire even without killing (so you can build the faction engine without charging to your death), hand a second model inspiring in one shoot-charge activation, and their Lethal 5+ Seek Light beats cover and tunnels. Treat a Cremator as "a Reliquarius with good melee". The default TP1 +1-APL benediction usually goes on a Cremator to threaten TP2. Skip the second Cremator into Breachers (they drop to a 2/2 profile).
- **Insidiant Warriors — the flex backbone; when in doubt, add a Warrior.** Silent full-range crossbow chip plus a 4/5 shock mace (+1 crit damage inspired). They out-value Cremators on open boards and into marines (their crit damage matters at 9–10 wounds), and you can happily run 4–5 of them. Basic Warrior crossbows are weak *uninspired* — don't burn 3 APL firing a 2/4 bow; they're a backup threat, not a primary plan.

## TacOp Selection
*You lean Crit-primary — the team's Security/Seek set is weak, so pick a TacOp you can score on the side while playing Crit.*

- **Plant Banner — your most plannable pick, but map-dependent.** Place it as little forward as possible, toward the opponent's uncommitted side, on a spot you can base-block or (better) a vantage point that jumpers/burrowers hate. Get the Censor to 3 APL to move-dash-plant last activation so she counts as 4 and no single model can steal it; babysit it with ~3 sisters incl. Censor/Abjuror. **Don't** take Banner into teams that yoink or overwhelm it — Kroot, Ratlings, Murderwing, Raveners, fast/high-activation teams. [strong consensus]
- **Dominate — premier pick, and you may be one of the best Dominate teams.** Don't chase it; play Crit, cover the objectives, and let your safe silent crossbows land the *last-activation* killing blow to bank tokens while your huge survivability keeps the token-carrier alive on 1–2 wounds. Incapacitating a 12+ wound enemy banks two tokens, so elite targets pay double. Especially strong into Seek/Security teams and wide shooty teams that must come to you (Arbites, Aquilons, dwarves). [strong consensus]
- **Envoy — strong when you out-activate, and into elites / low-activation teams.** Move the envoy on your last activation so it's safe and can't be stolen; a Cremator is the best envoy (scores and self-inspires, so you profit even if it dies). Often "playing for 4." Frequently the best pick into Raveners (hard for them to block both flanks). Weak when you're out-activated, and effectively dead on Into-the-Dark Tomb World.
- **Martyrs — a niche side pick, not a primary.** Only worth it into genuinely killy teams where you'll lose mid-point models anyway: put an already-inspired 3-APL Cremator on the centre at the very end of TP1 (dying is fine), and score while playing Crit. It's bad when you're winning (they won't feed you kills) and bad against late-scoring/recon opponents who simply pass through TP2; it effectively concedes a kill rank, so a "6" is really worth ~4.
- **Route — fringe; only on a wide open board versus a slow opponent** where you can go full-aggro. Otherwise skip.
- **Sweep & Clear — trap; skip.** It costs AP, its "clear" needs the objective uncontested, and it now only scores off the centre or the opponent's objective (not yours) — it competes with Banner for the same requirements and loses. Even pilots who like the playstyle wish it were good.

## Equipment Selection
- **Saintly Relics — always take.** ~30% to block a die when inspired (vs ~16% needing a 6 uninspired) — another reason to stay inspired. Two blocks per battle for the whole team, so plan them; roll it for essentially every damage die going through rather than hoarding it for "important" ones. It can't block Dev wounds (they resolve first), and once you roll the block die you must use it — don't roll against a crit you don't need to stop. It also throws opponents' math off hard. [strong consensus]
- **Psyk-out Grenades — near-auto-take.** 6" silent Seek Blast with a stun; on a 3+ stun test **every** operative caught also takes damage (halved dice, or full dice vs actual psykers) — a D3 stun-and-chip even against non-psykers, from conceal without stopping your crossbows. Great vs Fellgor (stun + chip to kill in two). Drop them only against teams that permanently ignore stun (Angels of Death, Deathwatch, Wolf Scouts) — but if the enemy pays CP each turn to ignore it, still take them to tax their CP. [strong consensus]
- **Vocifera Mortis — take on close boards; commonly dropped on the open.** In Tomb World / Gallowdark it lets you trigger a death-benediction on the exact sister you need without contorting positioning (and a good opponent will try to deny your Martyrdom targets). On open boards you can measure through walls and get visibility for free, so it's a crutch — drop it for ladders.
- **Smoke — take into shooting-heavy teams, hordes and vantage maps.** Holy Resilience doesn't proc on shooting and you can't deny obscuring, so smoke buys survival and forces melee — and you can still shoot out of it.
- **Krak / Frag Grenades — situational, non-Imperium only.** They give three otherwise-gunless models a ranged threat and can change a breakpoint before a charge, but have no native rerolls. Frags are deceptively good (the team has no other Blast). You can make grenades Ceaseless/Relentless only via the **Fervent Hate** firefight ploy (it isn't limited to datacard weapons) — the ceaseless *benediction* does not apply to them — so they're best into Chaos/xenos, poor into Imperium.
- **Light Barricades — usually the terrain pick.** Real cover, two of them, and they deny enemy equipment placements; block a centre objective so the enemy can't self-cover there, then stage to toast + contest last activation (huge on Tomb World centre objectives with no cover). Take razor wire only to lock a specific flank or banner spot (3" tax, no cover, one piece); it's also handy to disrupt a Ravener tunnel line. Heavy barricades must sit within your deploy zone and are rarely worth it; ladders are for offensive climbing; skip the Breaching Charge (a melta pistol breaches for 1 AP).
- **Auto-flagellator — skip.** A once-per-turn, non-guaranteed 4+ self-damage "inspire" that wrecks your favourable 9-wound breakpoints, can drop you below the threshold where Holy Resilience matters, and hands damage-on-charge teams free chip. Its "best" uses (flagging the Denuncia, who doesn't care about the wounds) still aren't good ones.

## Faction Rule Advice
- **Inspiration is the whole engine — spread it fast and wide, and never let an uninspired sister be exposed.** Damage reduction, the 2d6 Saintly Relics, longer charges, the death-benediction and most ploys all key off being inspired. Inspiring is permanent (it doesn't fall off at turning points). [strong consensus]
- **Standard TP1 "inspo train":** Superior inspires the Reliquarius → Reliquarius moves onto the home objective and inspires a third model within 6" → (optionally) Denuncia strips an inspiration to hand out a benediction. Inspire priority runs to models that will be charged (for DR), Warriors (crit damage), the Superior (Dev 2) and the Abjuror (easy to forget, since she castles). Hold off inspiring the Superior until ~TP3 so you keep early CP for ploys.
- **Inspiration is usually worth more than a benediction — don't strip it off a live frontliner early.** The TP1 "strip to convert" step is often a trap; only strip inspiration you've parked on a reserve model you're ready to lose.
- **Benediction priority:** +1 APL is the best generalist (charge-fight-shoot, reposition-shoot-mission, wider threat range — usually onto a Cremator TP1), but ceaseless or a clutch heal is often better into elites, and the free **dash** benediction is game-warping for threat projection (a zooming Mortisanctus). With nine activations you rarely *need* the extra APL — spend it on tempo (confirm a kill, lock a point, open a door for a charge), not a throwaway mission chip. The death-benediction reaches anyone within 6" **or** visible; note the Superior/Denuncia/Reliquarius *active* abilities need 6" **and** visible — a common, game-losing positioning trap. Benedictions resolve on removal (end of action), so a Blast/Torrent's benedictions all trigger after the action resolves; you can heal a survivor of the same blast but can't dash a model out of it. Denuncia's strip cannot hand out the dash benediction.
- **Martyrdom is situational, not a suicide plan — your nuns want to live.** Try to keep a sister alive when her death would help the opponent; only send-to-die when the death enables a strong benediction play, since sisters "love to survive things they shouldn't."
- **Weapons of the Witch Hunters (null field):** an enemy operative within range of any Celestian operative cannot benefit from, be selected for, or use any psychic rule — even if the psyker itself is outside the range; psychic rules have zero effect inside that distance. It's distance-only, ignores visibility, and measures through walls on open boards (but not through the special close-board walls). Most opponents just bench their psyker; don't build around this rule, but exploit it to switch off psychic damage-reduction auras by parking a sister in range.
- **Null-field / stat-change timing:** the Censor bubble only bites when an enemy *starts* an action within range — a charge that begins outside is unaffected, but it disrupts fall-backs, repositions and other charges. Grow it to 2" (enough for most games — that's break-cover / point-blank range) rather than paying APL to reach full range. The only mid-move exception is your own movement ploy.

## Ploys
- **Command Re-roll:** you're rerolls-poor (no always-on attack reroll, and ceaseless is dead vs Imperium), so spam the CP defence reroll — pilots use it far more than the offensive ploys. Bank CP toward the late turns (up to ~8 by TP4 when a passive opponent won't engage), then spend on rerolls/relics/ploys in the decisive turns.
- **Zealous Ultimatum (Mortisanctus' challenge):** always challenge when able. You rarely get the *permanent* +1 attack (if you're clearly going to kill them they just take the −1) — its real value is making a fight safer, punishing the coward who declines when another sister fights them later, and the team-wide −1 attack it hands out (good vs +1-charge-damage teams, Goremongers, Nemesis Claw). The token persists even if Morti dies.

### Strategic Ploys
- **Holy Resilience (damage reduction) — the team-defining ploy; play it most rounds after TP1.** It reduces crits too, shifting your breakpoints hard (a chainsword now needs three hits; an inspired Abjuror needs three crits through to die). Only works in melee/retaliation on an inspired model — it does nothing against shooting, and can be blunted by an opponent spamming their own defence reroll. [strong consensus]
- **Suffering & Sacrifice (Balanced-when-wounded):** situational but a solid second strategy ploy — worth it against anything that damages you on the charge (blades, Murderwing, Goremongers, Nemesis Claw) or shoots you with something that won't kill, so a dying sister is "part of the plan." The Balanced-when-wounded condition is why it's rated middling, not the reroll itself.
- **Suspect & Eliminate:** okay if free (via an inspired Denuncia), not worth paying for. It's weaker now that being inspiring already grants Severe (which no longer stacks with Punishing), and you rarely leave Denuncia inspired. It shines on close boards where a stack of Lethal-5 shooters + melee can fish crits.
- **Wrathful Determination:** low value — largely pointless unless you're going loud with the whole team.

### Firefight Ploys
- **Unshakeable Pursuit (+1" charge) — a top ploy, and free on an inspiring Superior** (every Celestian firefight ploy names the acting operative, so inspire her early to bank free ploys). It out-charges even terrain-ignoring teams, warps the game just by forcing the opponent to respect the extra range, and it strips **all** movement modifiers (ignore injury, enemy null bubbles, haywire/nano-mines mid-move). Can't be used on a counteract.
- **Glory to the Martyrs (strike-on-death) — huge deterrent value.** Its best use is charging an almost-dead sister in as an ~8-damage missile that also grants a benediction; opponents burn 3 APL to shoot you at 3+ saves rather than risk being struck on death. Combines with Saintly Relics to make enemies refuse fights, and chains with Faith & Fury (both trigger on the crit, before removal).
- **Fervent Hate (ceaseless/relentless) — great in every matchup it applies to,** and the flamer is its best user (reroll all fails). It also works on grenades (not limited to datacard weapons). Its limit — Imperium-keyword targets get nothing — is a big reason Imperium elites are hard. Each Blast/Torrent shot is a separate sequence, so less value there.
- **Faith & Fury (D3 damage in a 2" bubble):** decent for buying a breakpoint you can't otherwise reach, and good into hordes — it hits each *other* operative within 2" (not the one you struck), and can even trigger your own mid-fight Martyrdom heal. Underwhelming otherwise.

## Terrain

### Volkus / Octarius (open)
- **Skew toward Warriors over Cremators** — the silent full-range crossbow and +1 crit damage pay off where sightlines are long; Seek Light matters less.
- **Drop Vocifera Mortis for ladders** — you can measure benedictions through walls and get visibility for free, so it's a crutch; ladders give near-guaranteed value to a melee team.
- **Plant Banner on a vantage point** — burrowers and jumpers hate contesting up there, and open maps push the banner toward the middle (5" board-edge exclusion), so you need room to base-block it.
- **The Censor's null field rarely reaches onto vantage** (it can't climb mid-charge), so don't rely on it vertically; a big enemy model parked on vantage can be unreachable in melee.
- **Vantage double-shooting is the main threat** — a Conceal model is still targetable if the shooter sees both sides of its base, so tuck the base against terrain to stay obscured, stage *around* the enemy heavy so the angle can't be got, and use Smoke on exposed models. Getting shot in cover isn't too bad with the 3+ save.
- **Leave the Reliquarius on a home objective** — home objectives are usually safe on the open — and hide inspiration-generators behind a tall heavy barricade to dodge seek-light snipes.

### Tomb World / Gallowdark (close quarters)
- **Skew toward two Cremators** — many centre objectives have no cover, so the Lethal 5+ flamer shines and their short range matters less.
- **Vocifera Mortis is near-auto** — the special walls block measuring, so it's often the only way to route a benediction to the sister you need.
- **Silent shooting at doors is non-reciprocal** — you get the shot and they don't, which is strong when setting up against a Guarding enemy.
- **Block a centre objective with a light barricade** so the enemy can't self-cover there, then stage to toast + contest it on your last activation.
- **The inferno/melta pistol earns its slot** as a 1-AP wall breach; take Suspect & Eliminate here (stacked Lethal-5 shooting + melee); Envoy is effectively dead on Into-the-Dark.
- **Repeatedly operating a hatch shuts down enemies who climb APL through open doors** (e.g. Wreckas).

## Generic Advice
- **Core loop: castle vs elites, accelerate vs hordes.** Against elites hang back, run the inspiration/benediction engine and let your survivors ramp into "9-wound elites" by TP4 — the pressure is on the opponent to come to you. Against hordes, take casualties early and bring the fight so you don't lose to attrition. [strong consensus]
- **Feed one or two sisters into the frontline at a time,** rotating damaged models out and healing while standing on points; the next in line replaces her when she dies. Trade at a rate you can profit from — you heal off dying models and take reduced damage while inspired. [strong consensus]
- **Deny a point rather than roll for it — denying is worth as much as scoring and needs less dice.** When you're ahead, interact as little as possible so the dice can't ruin your plan.
- **Shoot before you bonk, then charge.** Chip a target with a silent crossbow/flamer to pick up inspiring and lower a breakpoint before committing melee (remember to flip the token to Engage before the shoot-charge-fight). Into marines, crossbows do surprising damage (~6 average, no crackback) and set up your 4/4 beat-sticks.
- **Deployment and TP1 win games.** Pre-measure threat ranges (charge 8+1"; repo-shoot 6+2/6+3+2), decide what you want staged for the top of TP2, and place barricades to enable that stage; threaten the far edges of objectives where the enemy must stand to score. Many losses are lost in deployment.
- **You're tougher than you look** — only a melta or a genuinely good weapon reliably one-shots a sister; a 3/4 bolter + Piercing 1 only kills ~48% shooting twice. Don't panic-play as if the girls are fragile.
- **Win-condition math:** hunt net-positive trades (2-for-2 TP2, 3-for-3 TP3) and keep total combats low — you have better one-turn tricks but not the wound count to survive below-average rolls, so control how many dice you're rolling.
- **The team's ceiling is TacOp selection and execution, plus heavy visibility/aura management** — it feels like a big mental load at first but simplifies once you stop forcing a "perfect" TP1–2 opening. Declare cover lines and reach up front; movement discipline is competitively load-bearing.
