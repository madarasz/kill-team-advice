# Battleclade - Advice

## TL;DR
- **Battleclade is a reactive counter-punch team, not a proactive gunline.** [strong consensus] Turtle up, hard-focus the crit op, bank easy Retrieval points, and punish enemy mistakes with Network Counteract. Win low-scoring games by a few points.
- **Don't stage forward.** [strong consensus] Let the enemy spend AP walking to you; always measure ranges so nothing of yours is chargeable. Getting tabled early means you were too aggressive.
- **The Technoarchaeologist is the team.** [strong consensus] It's your fast solo scorer, ceaseless/accurate dispenser, and (hitting on 3s) a real melee threat. Use its Strategic Gambit to reposition onto objectives, score, and flip back to conceal. "When you use it, it should feel like cheating."
- **Master the Noospheric Network.** Network Counteract to shoot is your core tempo tool and dodges Guard (except the Underseer's own commanded counteract). Set up ~3 reciprocal threats each round so you can swing back no matter who wins initiative.
- **Accuracy is the weakness** — most guns hit on 4+, so **ceaseless is near-mandatory** on any shot. Elites and other low-model shooting teams are your best matchups; anything that out-activates you (hordes, 9-10+ models) is your worst. Rule of thumb: enemy >9-10 operatives = bad, <7 = good.
- **High skill ceiling.** Plan the whole turning point before your first activation. Expect to lose your first several games (~6 to learn, ~15 for a good grip).

## Operative Selection
*Order: staples first, matchup-flex in the middle.*

- **Battleclade Technoarchaeologist** — the centerpiece and de-facto 4-APL model. Very fast, good gun, and now a genuine melee threat (Servo-Arc Claw hits on 3s — 3 crits deletes an 8-wound elite). Runs around scoring by itself, hands out up to two reroll (ceaseless) tokens per turning point that last the rest of the game, and uses its Strategic Gambit (Seeker of Divine Arcana) to reposition, do a mission action, and flip back to conceal. Losses trace to games where the Technoarchaeologist "never really executed." [strong consensus]
- **Servitor Underseer** — the other anchor piece; plan the board around where it stages. It enables all your out-of-sequence plays (Network Override, commanded counteracts, extra Transfer Power) and farms CP with its Datacoronal Accumulator action. Keep it tucked and safe (often on Engage/conceal) so you don't waste its APL, especially when out-activated; protect it with the Technomedic. Its own gun is a fine chip weapon (rending can spike to 2+ crits) but it's high-variance.
- **Technomedic Servitor** — high-value in most matchups and frequently the game's MVP, especially into shooting teams. Revives cost no APL loss and you can Network Counteract to heal twice; two d3+3 heals or a rez per game usually wins. It's also one of your better melee bodies. Keep it within ~3" of a gun/heavy servitor behind a barricade to build a near-unremovable "non-chargeable bubble." **Drop it for a 4th gun (3rd flamer) only into pure-melee teams** that hug-and-kill (Goremongers, Blooded). [strong consensus on the medic/gun swap being matchup-driven]
- **Gun Servitor** — better than the Combat Servitor thanks to Network Counteract flexibility; the melta profile is your reliable piercing answer to elites and tanks. Stage heavies forward as damage-soaks (a bare servitor dies in one fight; a heavy in front changes that) — but hold them back vs profiles with 5 melee attacks that punch through your damage reduction.
- **Breacher Servitor** — hits on 3s, a reliable finisher (P2 within 2") and a wide-area threat via its breach ability; consistently rated among the best value pieces. Use it to clean up targets others chipped and to breach onto enemy home objectives.
- **Auto-Proxy Servitor** — an anchor/support piece, not a scorer. Its auras are "basically essential": Saturate (strips enemy cover saves), ignore-obscuring, and seek light. Position it to see your targets; it can't watch two objectives without backing off and dropping the auras. It also has Lethal 5+ in melee (good on a charge).
- **Combat Servitor** — the flex body. Default is the flamer (igniter); **flamers beat the Phosphor Blaster** for most players (Blast 1" is easy to dodge and it whiffs often). Reserve phosphors for open-board vantage (Noospheric buff + vantage = Acc 2) or dice-volume into 5+ save hordes. Magnetize arms so you can flex flamer/phosphor/melta and the medic slot per matchup.

**Default build:** all specialists + a melta + two flamers, medic behind them. Swap the medic for a 3rd flamer into melee; drop the melta for a 3rd flamer into squishy anti-pierce hordes.

## TacOp Selection
*Order: best → trap. Battleclade draws from Recon and Infiltration; the shared Seek & Destroy tac ops (Dominate/Sweep) are not in its pool.*

Select **Crit Op as primary** — only when you're near-certain to score the full 5 (e.g. the opponent is scared to contest center, elites, Transmission). Otherwise Retrieval's guaranteed 3-4 is safer.

- **Retrieval** — the default primary and comfort pick. [strong consensus] Almost always banks a safe 4 points because this team can reach the enemy home; ~95% guaranteed. Better vs elites (fewer, harder-worked tokens elsewhere). The Edmonton Open winner played Retrieval every game.
- **Steal Intelligence** — the go-to vs teams with 7+ operatives, melee/close-range teams, and elves. [strong consensus] Synergizes with **Duty of Reclamation**: once tokens are scattered the whole team gets constant free CP rerolls. Very hard to score vs low-model elites — take Retrieval there instead.
- **Scout Enemy Movement** — niche, high-variance, and usually a trap. [strong consensus] It forces you onto conceal and forgoes offense TP3/4, and you often end up killing the very models you needed to scout. Real uses are narrow: vs Canoptek (tall walkers can't hide), vs Envoy/Banner or 10+ model campers (mirror their scoring), or on close boards with doors for a quick 1-2 points. Take it mainly when you expect to lose, to minimize the loss. Blowing the Underseer's first activation to scout three enemies is widely criticized as abdicating your threat ranges.
- **Plant Devices** — a weak pick for such a static team. It can't score before TP2, needs to win TP2 initiative, and caps around 3 VP; repeatedly cited as a poor result.

## Equipment Selection
*Order: staples first, situational later.*

- **Electromantic Capacitors** — as close to an auto-take as it gets; your best survivability tool. Strong into shooting teams, elves, and moderate melee (a melee death moves from ~90% to ~76%, and forcing a double-Fight can stop a mission action or a double-kill). **Skip it vs melee that kills you regardless** — profiles with 5 attacks that punch straight through the damage reduction (Raveners, Nemesis Claw pieces, Goremongers).
- **Covert Guises** — high value, taken most of the time. Forces good opponents to respect your space and stage back (a cascade advantage across turning points), and lets the Technoarchaeologist do a full reposition ending near your DZ — over a wall into a stronghold, or to reach a vantage for the free omniscanner. Loses value vs hordes that out-activate you; unreliable for a TP1 alpha (much better TP2 or with ceaseless).
- **Neurocyclic Reserve Cells** — lets you Transfer Power for 0 AP once per turning point with no penalty; widely rated an auto-take, key to the fall-back-then-Network-Counteract-shoot answer to melee. (Some veterans consider it a crutch and rarely fire it — bring it for action-heavy crit/tac ops.)
- **Concealed Apparatus** — situational surprise piercing: once per battle a Combat or Gun Servitor fires a weapon option it didn't equip — most often the "pocket melta," an extra P2 shot into 3+ save teams (marines, dwarves) or Exodites. Good for cracking a target from a range the enemy didn't plan for.

**Universal killzone equipment** (heavily used):
- **Light & heavy barricades** — staples. A heavy barricade on your home lets the medic sit behind it and negates enemy move-dash-shoot; light barricades create inside-outside "unchargeable" staging and block charge lanes. Position models so they're inside/outside and can't be charged.
- **Razor wire** — close-board staple; lengthwise in a corridor makes charging near-impossible.
- **Comms Device** — situational; good when you turtle the Underseer (or Auto-Proxy) all game so you never have to move the network's origin, and specifically strong vs Raveners.
- **Smoke** — pair with Covert Guises vs out-activating shooting teams; screen the Technoarchaeologist so nothing pulls you off a point without committing two operatives.
- **Krak grenades** — mostly skip. [strong consensus] You already have plenty of native piercing (pocket melta, melta, arc, heavy bolter), and the alternative slots materially change the tactical flow while kraks just add more of the shooting you already have. Exception: give them to the medic if you want it to have a shooting presence.

## Matchups
*Order: favored → hardest. This channel is shared with Raveners players, so Raveners is a common (and favorable) opponent.*

|---|---|---|
| **Death Guard / Plague Marines** | Favored | "A nightmare for them." Medic saves 1-HP models and purges poison, Underseer double-dashes them to safety; loads of meltas; you easily control their poor threat range; **Auto-Proxy negates Cloud of Flies.** |
| **Nemesis Claw** | Favored | **Auto-Proxy cancels their obscuring/faction rule**, System Exorcism counters their vox, Capacitors help vs their damage. Push one Combat Servitor up each flank, everyone else near the DZ; wait to activate the Underseer until they're on counteracts. Beware **Proclivity for Murder** (charge → fight → charge → blast, plus the Screecher shutting off your ceaseless). |
| **Deathwatch / Angels of Death** (shooting elites) | Favored, play cagey | You out-activate them and the pocket melta over-performs; Take secondaries that don't require kills, wait them out with the Technoarchaeologist scanning, then punish. Some players find Deathwatch a run-away matchup, so lean on out-activation and low-scoring rather than trading. |
| **Raveners** | Favored | Their weakness is tac ops: bank ~4 easy Retrieval while they must come to you, then use seek light to make center unsafe and go even on crit. Spread out, give the tunnel a wide berth (huge bases), never breach your own points. Comms Device double-dash a Combat Servitor onto their tunnel marker (exactly 2" per dash to avoid Tremorscythe). They hate flamers and saturate. |
| **Exodites** | Favored (esp. open) | Kill the clanblade/dragon rider with the melta TP2 and it's nearly over; out-body them (5 > 3) and smash them on crit. On open they can't hide from free Transfer Power fights; burn CP TP1 on Iron Soul + Noospheric Possession. 4× phosphor for dice volume into their 5+ saves is viable. |
| **Salvagers** | Favored, grindy | Outrange with double-dash if they don't commit; melta on your last activation for a kill + activation advantage; take shock equipment (they're weak in melee). Keep the Underseer out of jetpack range. Let their Jump Pack Warrior yolo in and tank the krak with -1 damage + medic — it's worth more to them alive. |
| **Mandrakes** | Slightly favored | "Not a bad matchup." Play patiently, use the Technoarchaeologist to score mid and get a kill each turning point, keep Iron Soul up, bring Capacitors. **Drop the melta — they ignore piercing.** |
| **Scout / Wolf Scouts** | Even | Ignore the sniper on guard (it can't shoot you), fortify home with a heavy barricade, breach the stronghold wall and pop the Technoarchaeologist onto the objective. Iron Soul wrecks their shotgun reliability; a 19" melta to the face goes a long way; light barricades to build the slow move-dash path. Capacitors carry the melee. |
| **Celestian Insidiants / Sacresants** (Sisters) | Even | Kill the off-objective / uninspired operatives to wreck their scoring; the Technoarchaeologist is a power piece (deletes an Insidiant). Heavy Bolter + Capacitors + medic on center; Iron Soul carries you through melee (but does nothing vs stake throwers). Their even model count can stall your good activations. |
| **Warp Coven** | Even | Stage TA/Breacher/melta inside your stronghold, breach a corner, keep a heavy barricade at home out of Temporal-Flux reach. Prioritised Acquisition center every turn, play Retrieval so the Technoarchaeologist grabs and returns through the breach. |
| **Spectre Squad** | Even, tricky | **Network Counteract doesn't trigger their Fieldcraft reaction** — move up in conceal then NC-shoot with a flamer. Contest mid but don't open fire unless forced (they out-activate you); Auto-Proxy saturate removes their double cover saves. |
| **Stealth Suits / XV26 / Aquilons** (shooting) | Even → hard | **Medic is MVP**; play passive, control home + mid and never cross the centerline. NCA-healing enables long trades. Reposition-dash + Divine Arcana onto the point TP2 to deny pickup, smoke on the Technoarchaeologist. Watch enemy Blast weapons. |
| **Fellgor** | Even | Deploy a flamer to match wherever they place the shaman; a TP1 alpha that frenzies the shaman removes their super-conceal bubble and likely wins. |
| **Chaos Cults** | Debated | One line: deploy in the open (they lack long-range shooting), Guises dash-dash-repo-shoot (~16" threat) + NCA-shoot a flamer into cultists TP1. But they have last-activation + inevitability, so you can't out-cage them; when out-activated, take ground and lean on Iron Soul (a good phosphor case). |
| **Vespids** | Unfavorable | One of your two hardest. | **Exaction Squad** | Very hard | The Nuncia taxes Steal Intel/Retrieval, so Scout Enemy Movement gains outsized value. Iron Soul to avoid two-hits; watch the dog's 10" charge and its fall-back-denial debuff. The Technoarchaeologist's free mission action bypasses the increased action cost. |
| **Inquisitorial Agents** | Very hard | Near-unwinnable; treat as a max-points-dropped matchup in tournaments. Their melta/plasma delete your forward heavies and Intense Scrutiny dictates staging. ## Faction Rule Advice
*Noospheric Network / Network Counteract (NCA) / Transfer Power.*

- **Network Counteract to shoot is your core tempo tool — do it constantly** ("ceaseless > accurate"). It lets you threaten an extra shot from an expended operative (the opponent must react), shoot twice before they respond, or fix positioning. Doubleshoots that kill two marines in a turning point are huge swings.
- **NCA does not trigger Guard** (it isn't an action / isn't during an activation), so you can NC-shoot a Guard operative unpunished — then activate to conceal. **Exception:** the Underseer's *own commanded* Network Counteract is an action the enemy can respond to.
- **Transfer Power is free** and no longer costs APL: once per friendly Servitor activation, spend 1 AP to Transfer Power and perform a 1 AP action free — but the operative **cannot move more than 2"** during that free action. A -APL/stunned servitor cannot Transfer Power or counteract at all (fix the -APL with System Exorcism first).
- **Underseer Network Override** can be used twice per activation, each on a **different** friendly operative — to Dash it, make an Auto-Proxy network counteract, or grant a free Dash. Use it to project two threats or set up a charge (dash a heavy, then have it counteract-charge). You can no longer stack two dashes on the same servitor.
- **Datacoronal Accumulator** — the Underseer rolls D3 against contested objective markers to gain 1 CP; use it whenever the Underseer has nothing better to do. This team is far better with a lot of CP because its ploys are so strong, so farm it.
- **Auto-Proxy scan-and-shoot** — the Technoarchaeologist and Auto-Proxy don't need line of sight to scan within 8" (even through Volkus walls); scan an enemy in cover with the Auto-Proxy, then Transfer Power to shoot it immediately with saturate/seek light stripping its protection. Avoid shooting anything without at least Accurate 1 + Ceaseless.
- **Order flipping** — use NCA to flip a model back to conceal after a risky shot (a "discount ghostshroud"), and you can Network Counteract even when the opponent has no ready operatives left.
- **Reciprocal threats** — because you can re-activate expended operatives, set up ~3 threats each round so that whoever the enemy hits, you still execute with the other two and counter-punch. Trade generic servitors for their power pieces.

## Ploys

- **Command Re-roll** — pairs with Duty of Reclamation and ceaseless for reliable objective work even without Accurate.

### Strategic Ploys
*Order: best → situational.*

- **Incantation of the Iron Soul** — the survival core; keep it up "every time all the time." [strong consensus] Subtract 1 from inflicted damage on a 4+; watch teams struggle to kill your 8-wound bodies. Does nothing vs stake/thrown-weapon effects.
- **Prioritised Acquisition** — out-APL 3-APL teams and hold points with a contesting 4-APL body; the standard way to squeeze extra APL onto an objective (walk the Technoarchaeologist on via Divine Arcana, do a mission action, run away), and it grants extra attacks to make heavies threaten 8-9 wound operatives. Applies immediately, not just in the firefight phase. [strong consensus]
- **Duty of Reclamation** — a reroll while on objectives; the standout bang-for-buck ploy, especially with Steal Intel scattering markers so the whole team rerolls. Great on grenades and combat. [strong consensus]
- **Noospheric Possession** — situational; burn CP on it TP1 (alongside Iron Soul) vs teams that buff themselves, e.g. Exodites.

### Firefight Ploys
*Order: best → situational.*

- **Remote Access** — perform a mission action within 4" without line of sight or control range (measure the 4" from the actual marker), as long as no enemy contests it. Retrieve or plant from inside a stronghold, or while a servitor sits safely in melee. Only the two tech-priests qualify, and you cannot Pick Up Marker with it.
- **Servile Surrogacy** — redirect a shot or fight off a key model (the Technoarchaeologist) onto a nearby servitor within 3", which the medic then revives. Inside a stronghold it's especially strong — the surrogate fights first at 3+ and keeps combat-support to-hit. (On a Shoot action the servitor is only in cover/obscured if the original target was.)
- **System Exorcism** — situational; removes mission-pack rule effects that would otherwise hamper you. Useful in specific killzones/missions, but no longer strips self-inflicted or applied -1 APL.

## Terrain

### Volkus / Octarius (open)
- Put a Gun Servitor or phosphor on a 2nd-story vantage: cross-map zoning, Scout-Enemy-Movement visibility, and Noospheric + vantage = Acc 2. A phosphor deep in a back corner is a permanent cross-map threat not worth the enemy killing.
- Guises + Network Override + NCA movement can put multiple bodies onto a vantage stronghold TP1; screen charge lanes with razor wire and tap the enemy home with the Breacher.
- GW's stock Octarius maps favor the clade — breach-point/stronghold play is proactive and plannable, and seek-light shots work more often.
- Volkus door play: stand at your home door, repo the Technoarchaeologist onto the enemy home to steal points or shoot first-activation, then run back behind the door out of door-fight range.
- Shooting teams / Exodites: sit on vantage and shoot; floor-is-lava + Scout Enemy Movement works because they can't shoot conceal-on-vantage and get only one fight per activation.

### Tomb World / Gallowdark (close quarters)
- Guard with flamers, plus dash-dash-move-Guard, creates a lot of space; hold firing lanes with On Guard.
- Control big rooms and hallways with the Auto-Proxy + Arc, popping the Technoarchaeologist in and out to score crit; dominate rooms rather than pushing through doors.
- Teleport pads: NCA to switch places with an enemy on the opposite pad, then shoot; block a pad by putting one operative on it and one touching it.
- Scout Enemy Movement can nick 1-2 easy points on maps with doors (but TW2 is a poor Scout map).
- vs Raveners: chop the map into discrete pockets, never breach your own points, guard with flamers, and NCA "shut the door" to make it an AP tax; a Comms Device to guarantee heavy-bolter positioning is great here.
- Covert Guises lets the Technoarchaeologist repo over a wall into a stronghold or open a door; a concealed melta can breach a wall on an NCA shot.
- Servile Surrogacy shines inside a stronghold — redirect the fight onto a heavy servitor inside so it fights first at 3+ and keeps combat-support.
- vs Exodites this is worse than open — they close on you before presenting a target.

## Generic Advice
- **Play reactive.** [strong consensus] Battleclade counter-punches; wait for the opponent's error or set a trap, don't lead with your own offense. The game is decided TP3/4 — aim to win low-scoring by a few points.
- **Never touch your own home objective early** — Remote-Access it instead of walking a body back.
- **Set up ~3 reciprocal threats each round** so a lost initiative still lets you shoot/NCA-shoot with the other two; dial down to ≤2 (ideally 0, with a lone front gun servitor) vs teams that out-activate you.
- **The Technoarchaeologist is your symmetry-breaker** — score, retrieve, hand out ceaseless, and dip in and out via the Strategic Gambit. Grabbing an early mid-board retrieval token (for team-wide free rerolls) is often better than gunning straight for the enemy home; save the aggressive alpha for when the enemy leaves models exposed.
- **Accuracy is your biggest weakness** — most guns hit on 4+, so place your Accurate-1 bubble and ceaseless correctly and don't shoot without them. If an enemy sits within 1" of your target you hit both with ceaseless + accurate.
- **You telegraph.** With 8 wounds and 4+ saves you get counter-punched hard when a setup fails, so plan the entire turning point before your first activation. Expect ~6 games to start learning the team and ~15 for a good grip; games run long, so watch the clock.
- **Guard from conceal to secure the last activation** is one of your biggest tools.
- **Threat range is larger than the 5" move suggests** — repositions, dashes, and Network Override let a melta or breacher project over a wide area, so elites that can't change order to counteract must respect it.
- **The alpha strike is a threat weapon, not a plan.** Covert Guises + an Underseer dash can push a melta/breacher far forward TP1, but it's high-variance and rarely executed cleanly; its real value is forcing opponents to deploy back and respect your space. The best players pull it maybe once a weekend.
