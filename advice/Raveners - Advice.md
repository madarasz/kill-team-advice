# Raveners - Advice

## TL;DR

- **Don't get shot — and never get shot twice.** ~18 wounds (Prime 19) with a 5+ save and no defensive re-roll ploy means a melta, plasma, or even a bolter deletes a bug. If you're taking fire, you misplayed. [strong consensus]
- **You are a slow-burn control team, not nidzilla. Hide TP1–2, "open the can" TP3–4.** Give up early scoring, stay cagey, then pop off when the tunnel is where you need it. Impatience is your worst matchup. [strong consensus]
- **Win by not dying and by denying, not by big scores.** Going from 2–3 losses to 0–1 losses per game is worth ~2 VP. There is essentially no play worth losing a bug over. [strong consensus]
- **Run all five specialists — never the plain Warrior.** [strong consensus]
- **Dominate is the default TacOp ~90–99% of games**, with Crit Op as your primary. Take Sweep & Clear only when the enemy refuses to interact. [strong consensus]
- **Metamorphic Flesh (D3 heal) is the one auto-take equipment.** [strong consensus]
- **The tunnel is your whole game.** Learn map distances, run it home→mid→enemy, keep markers 0–1 straight and TP3–4 flexible. Rotate wounded bugs back through it, keep fresh bodies forward.
- **Chip is the engine.** Poison, Burrowing Strike, Toxic Lunge, Death Frenzy shift enemies to breakpoints you can finish. Critical in every matchup where the enemy out-fights you.

## Operative Selection

You field one of each of the five specialists every game. Roles, best→worst by how much work they do:

- **Ravener Wrecker — your most important operative and best damage.** Most durable body (4+ save, reduces incoming 4+ damage by 1), even his tail out-averages most tools, and Crush can one-shot low-wound models (its bonus is a roll-off — unreliable, so don't bank on it). Use him aggressively into elites' faces rather than parking him home; he's also the best shot-tank and home anchor when you need one. No other bug fills the tank role. [strong consensus]
- **Ravener Felltalon — MVP aggressive melee in most games.** Lethal 5+ poison, shock, 6" silent tail, and Toxic Lunge for direct poke damage. Charge → Toxic Lunge → fight is the signature kill line. Toxic Lunge now needs visibility to the target and can't reach past enemies already in your control range — it no longer bites blind through walls, so position for a clear line. Still your safest way to tick down a target you don't want to fully commit into. [strong consensus]
- **Ravener Venomspitter — never dropped; the only real gun.** Full-move heavy blast profile, Lethal 5+, poison, and a melee profile as good as a Warrior's. Play it as a close-range "blapper," not a sniper — you have no way to force enemies to expose, so long-range shooting "isn't real." Shoot on a late activation, then flip to conceal or burrow. Keep it near the tunnel/ground, not parked on vantage. It high-rolls games open on its own into elites. [strong consensus]
- **Ravener Tremorscythe — the tunnel interrupt / area-denial deterrent.** Its Subterranean Ambush (interrupt an enemy that moves >2" and ends within 2" of the tunnel, in the firefight phase) is more a psychic threat than a tool — often you just remind the opponent it exists and they concede space. Also the bug you can most afford to lose, so it makes a fine early pointman. Keep it underground toward TP4, take the first genuinely good interrupt, protect it with Tunnel Lurkers. Note: if the interrupted model survives, the opponent finishes its activation and then activates again — weigh it. Does **not** trigger on Fly (Vespid) or a boost-only finish (Murderwing); a walk-then-boost that moves >2" first does.
- **Ravener Prime — support and objective control, not an attacker.** Its no-re-roll aura (within 3") forces "Russian roulette" on enemy attacks and is great into re-roll-reliant teams; it reduces the total contesting APL on a point by 1 and taxes/denies mission actions. Best floating on home or a key objective — opponents simply avoid it. Its CP roll-off (Synaptic Link) is worth rolling most turns when the Prime is safe, but the reward now needs the roll to come up twice as high or higher, so treat it as a bonus, not a plan — never gamble it when a bad result drops the Prime past a breakpoint.
- **Ravener Warrior — never take it.** Lethal 5+ only on wounded targets isn't worth losing a specialist's utility; every specialist brings more. Build one only as a spare model to measure with. [strong consensus]

## TacOp Selection

- **Dominate — the default, ~90–99% of games.** No APL cost, rewards killing and not dying, and it now hands you **2 tokens for incapacitating a Wounds 12+ enemy**, so it scores hard into elites. Rule of thumb: play Dominate until a game where you score 0–1 on it, then look at Sweep. **Critical current limitation: you can only cash tokens from friendly operatives that are in the killzone and not incapacitated — a bug in the tunnel can no longer score.** So cash at end of turn and burrow after, or surface a bug to cash; leave a low-HP bug in your home zone you can counteract-pop to cash. Its one weakness is opponents who refuse to engage. [strong consensus]
- **Sweep & Clear — the #2 pick and the answer to uninteractive/cagey teams** (all-Banshee Blades of Khaine, passive marines, Stealth Suits, keep-away Canoptek/Salvagers). You score off clearing the **centre or the opponent's objective (never your own)**, uncontested, plus controlling a swept marker — up to 2 VP/turn. Beeline the tunnel to mid, contest on-tunnel, and let the Tremorscythe interrupt secure the token. Don't force it (~1 game in 10). Best on control crit ops (Loot, Secure, Data, Energy Cells).
- **Track Enemy — situational secondary vs 5/6-model passive teams (Deathwatch especially, sometimes Canoptek/Exodites).** With Tunnel Lurkers you're always in cover, so pop out on the last activation and score for 2 with no risk. Weak vs Seek Light teams (you stop being a valid target-denier) and fiddly once revealed.
- **Rout — kill-dependent, for when you can't stay alive to hold** (no need to keep the scorer alive; 2 VP/turn cap). Fine vs Stealth Suits and hang-back elites on wide maps; bad vs 3+ melee ops, teams that rush you, or Deathwatch (they cross the centreline and flip the melee math).
- **Plant Devices — usually a trap.** You typically sacrifice 20% of your team YOLO-ing to the enemy home for ~3 real points. Only when the crit op is "hold at end of TP," you can plant early and survive, in the mirror, or specifically into Murderwing's "floor is lava" play.
- **Steal Intelligence — hampered by the fact you can't burrow while carrying the marker**, which kills your escape and costs APL on the worst-APL team. Take it only when you don't expect the carrier to survive, or for a TP2 grab-and-hide into low-model teams.
- **Avoid outright:** the pure-hold-contest ops that force early exposure with no fallback.

## Equipment Selection

- **Metamorphic Flesh (D3 heal) — the one true auto-take, every game.** Heals on activation (not counteract), negates poison, and is better on Raveners than most teams because you pop up, fight, burrow to safety, and heal while rotating a fresh bug in. Whiffs in games where you take 0 or 100-to-0 damage, but the insurance and breakpoint-shifting are worth it. [strong consensus]
- **Chromatospore Camouflage (double cover save) — situational, not a staple.** Its real value is forcing shooters to close within 2" (where you punish them), and it shines into Seek-Light-but-no-Saturate teams (Blades of Khaine, Spectre, Exaction on close quarters). Dead vs Saturate (Angels of Death, Deathwatch, Wrecka, Battleclade, grenade teams) and it does **not** stack with a vantage's improved save. If you're playing correctly and staying concealed, you often don't need it.
- **Acid Blood — take into melee-heavy / high-wound / damage-reduction teams; skip vs ranged and 7–8W teams.** Its job is shifting breakpoints (turning "2 crits + a hit" into "a crit + 2 hits" on a marine) and creating parry-or-die head-math for the opponent. It only procs on a 5+ and its single biggest failure is forgetting to roll it — literally keep the card by your dice tray. Can backfire by killing a model you wanted left alive.
- **Razor wire / barricades — strong for you specifically**, because you burrow up right under them: funnel enemies toward the tunnel, block charge lanes, wall off objectives, deny enemy staging and banner spots. Place razor wire on the centre objective if you win setup initiative for cover you can use and they can't. Don't wall off your own charge lanes.
- **Ladders — near-must on Volkus.** Stage from vantage, reach your tunnel through terrain, deny enemy floor-is-lava/vantage-banner play, and cancel the -2" move after unburrowing. Especially vs Security/banner teams.
- **Ammo Cache — okay, niche.** Put it near mid for aggressive Venomspitter pressure TP3/4 (shooting re-rolls combo with the blast); can trap you into a static, telegraphed position. Marginal on a single-shooter team.
- **Mines — good vs low-wound teams** (elves, Sisters, Goremongers) and to block doors/lanes on close-quarters maps.
- **Stun grenades — good into 2-APL and aggressive teams** (Wyrmblade, Vespid, Battleclade — a stunned model can't fall back or network-counteract); throwable on a counteract from conceal, and they extend your reach better than kraks.
- **Kraks — generally weak** (4 dice on 4s, no dice-fixing; going on Engage to throw them is a trap). Take only to fill an empty slot or as a pure vantage-camper threat on Volkus.
- **Smoke — weak now** (only helps vs P2, and Tunnel Lurkers already forces enemies within 2"); occasional value for a Venomspitter vantage-shot-then-smoke. Bad into Wreckas (feeds their points).
- **Heightened Senses (initiative re-roll) — usually the first drop.** Take it only when you lack the initiative-re-roll card and need to control TP3/4 initiative.

## Faction Rule Advice

- **Tunnel first, movement tool second: if a bug has no reason to be out, keep it in.** Plan the route before deployment but stay flexible; when in doubt run it up the middle. Baseline path **home → mid → enemy home**; once it covers mid you should never lose mid. Keep markers 0→1 as straight as possible and leave TP3–4 flexible to react to the board. [strong consensus]
- **Know your ranges off the tunnel:** "on the tunnel" (base + ~2.6" control) lets you burrow into/out of enemy control range — charge → fight → burrow denying counterattack; ~4.6" is counteract-charge / Writhe-escape range; burrow-charge threat is ~9.6". Keep ~62% of your base "behind" the tunnel so any 2" shooter is within charge range and you stay covered.
- **Burrow costs 1 AP, so you play like a pseudo-2-APL team — budget for it.** It's the same action up or down (you can't burrow up, act, and burrow down in one activation — the second burrow waits for a counteract). It is **not** a Move/Charge/Fall Back, so unburrowing into control range doesn't trigger charge reactions (retro-thrusters, overwatch) and you can land in melee on Conceal.
- **You can't burrow while carrying a marker** (Steal Intel, Energy Cells) — but Dominate *tokens* are fine (though you must now be above ground in the killzone to cash them).
- **Predatory Instincts: any bug can counteract regardless of order, on Conceal, can change order first, and gets a free Burrow if it performs no mission action** — so counteract → (flip to Engage) → shoot/fight → free burrow to safety. Never mission-action + burrow in the same counteract.
- **Rotation is the core mechanic:** the mid bug that eats a shot burrows away, the home bug burrows and re-emerges at mid, the injured bug retreats home to heal. This lets your 5 match an elite team's 5 forward bodies indefinitely.
- **Underground consequences:** a bug that dies in the tunnel drops no Confirm-Kill/poison marker (good on defense), **but any bug left underground at game end counts as incapacitated for the enemy Kill Op** — keep enough counteracts to surface everyone by end of TP4.
- **Don't marry the tunnel or your home objective**, especially into elites and slow teams — use the tunnel to *support* above-ground bugs and set up next-turn threats.

## Ploys

- **CP economy: you're CP-rich** (few strong ploys, and you give initiative to bank CP), so lean on Command Re-rolls for key fights — but save CP TP1–2 and don't blow it all on defence re-rolls.

### Strategic Ploys

- **Tunnel Lurkers — near-auto every turn.** Makes you a non-valid target on the tunnel unless the enemy comes within 2" (it is *not* a cover save — don't misplay it as one). It's how you don't get shot in the first place and how you force Tremorscythe interrupts. [strong consensus]
- **Death From Below — a "go-turn" ploy, not a staple; use TP3–4.** Grants Balanced when you unburrow/leave the tunnel and Ceaseless while on the tunnel (you get **both** if you burrow onto the tunnel and fight). Only applies when *you* fight, never on retaliation, and only that activation. Often a plain double-fight is better value. [strong consensus it's over-rated off the tunnel]
- **Whipcord Emergence — your defensive backup, but much weaker now.** It re-rolls one chosen defence-dice result value, not any of your defence dice, so a 5+ save with it is only a modest help — no longer close to Relentless. Keep it for when you must be near the tunnel against a shooty team, but don't build your survival around it; positioning and Tunnel Lurkers matter more.
- **Writhe Out of Sight — your escape/safety valve.** In the strategy phase, when a bug is within 2" of the tunnel, it gets its **full** move/fall-back to reach the line, then a free burrow (you must be able to burrow). Use it to pull an over-extended or wounded bug to safety after an aggressive last-activation score, and to rotate injured bugs back to heal. Stage aggressively knowing you can Writhe if you lose initiative.

### Firefight Ploys

- **Slithering Evasion — 1 CP and an AP (not a free charge).** Makes Fall Back a 1-AP action so you can burrow away, or charge out of the control range of the bug you started in — great for slipping past shield walls to reach a backline (Exaction can't deny it) or leaving a frenzied model to die. Best on the Venomspitter.
- **Subterranean Horror — fight-first on the tunnel.** You resolve first (works vs Guard and Repress), but it's still retaliation so no Death From Below re-rolls. Remember it when charged on the tunnel and to kill a Fellgor in-activation.
- **Burrowing Strike — core chip engine.** Hits everyone in your control range as you burrow (~D3+1, min 2, once per turn, requires burrowing up in the strategy phase); enemies clump for it. Double-fight turns come from Burrowing Strike + Death From Below. Can't combo with Toxic Lunge or while Writhing.
- **Death Frenzy — bank a CP for it**, especially vs Goremongers / to shift a breakpoint on death; a dying bug still can't score its Dominate tokens.

## Terrain

### Volkus / Octarius (open)

- **Vantage is what turns your hard matchups into your worst ones.** Any team that can BS-shoot from high ground — Deathwatch, Murderwing, Wrecka Krew, Aquilons/Kasrkin, Vespid — is far more dangerous here than in close quarters: they camp vantage, strip your Tunnel Lurkers light cover with Saturate, and stay unreachable while scoring. Pressure their home early, and consider Sweep & Clear or Plant Devices against vantage-banner campers. [strong consensus]
- **The Ravener Prime and Ravener Felltalon are too tall to end a move under most vantage ceilings — only the Ravener Wrecker, Ravener Venomspitter, and (sometimes) Ravener Tremorscythe fit.** Plan burrow spots around it: park the Wrecker or Venomspitter on objectives that sit under vantage, and against Exodites tunnel *under* vantage on purpose since their dinos can't follow. [strong consensus]
- **Ladders are an open-board staple and near-dead weight on close quarters.** Take them to reach or deny vantage, kill enemy "floor is lava" and vantage-banner play, and — placed within ~40mm+1" of the tunnel line — to climb straight up after burrowing (cancelling the -2" unburrow move). [strong consensus]
- **Double cover (Chromatospore Camouflage) is weak on Volkus** — it doesn't stack with a vantage's own cover benefit and does nothing when you're shot down from vantage. Prefer razor wire over barricades here.
- **Don't strand the Ravener Venomspitter on vantage — it just gets deleted.** Keep it near the tunnel to extend threat range; the one exception is running it up a vantage on TP1 to threaten enemy banner spots, then dropping back out of line of sight.
- **A heavy barricade on your home objective is especially valuable here**, because easy enemy vantage access threatens home — sit on the tunnel behind it.
- **The Ravener Tremorscythe charging onto a vantage enemy from Conceal** is a strong play that only exists on open, high-ground boards.
- **Cut the tunnel through stronghold corners** to guarantee an emergence point on either side, and watch that an enemy base doesn't body-block your exit when you surface just inside a stronghold.
- **Rout and Track Enemy are harder on open boards** — wide lanes and vantage let the enemy stay out of Rout range or deny a valid Track target from high ground.

### Tomb World / Gallowdark (close quarters)

- **The tunnel measures and burrows straight through Gallowdark / Into the Dark walls — skip doors entirely and pop directly into rooms.** This is the tunnel's best board family. Exception: it cannot pass Tomb World noctolith walls, so route around them there. [strong consensus]
- **Run the tunnel past doors and hatchways to farm Ravener Tremorscythe interrupts.** Moving through a door costs a minimum of ~3", forcing enemies over the 2" threshold — even Vespid (can't fly through hatchways) and Murderwing (must walk before boosting) trigger Subterranean Ambush. Burrow up and hatchway-fight from Conceal by treating the charge as a reposition. [strong consensus]
- **Thread the tunnel through different rooms and hug board edges.** Zigzag so you keep pop-up options on both sides of each wall (cut diagonally across a wall to choose which room you surface in); never run it into the center of a big room — you get crunched from three sides.
- **Tunnel Lurkers is far stronger here** — enemies can't hide on vantage, the light cover is more efficient, and you can kill several models clumped behind one door at once. Doubling bugs up in a small room leaves the enemy no space to move without triggering the Ravener Tremorscythe.
- **Placeable equipment lets you cage the board and tunnel under it** — but doors and breachable walls count as access points, so placement is restricted. Razor wire in front of a door blocks passage (drop a barricade on the far side if they clear it); take barricades on close boards, not Volkus.
- **Mines actually bite here**: narrow doors plus pillars force even small bases into a mine's control range (they simply dodge on Volkus, which has no pillars). Wall off teleporter rows on Tomb World to stop enemy teleport-drops.
- **Ravener Venomspitter door-frame trick**: park it just outside 1" of a door with the base offset to draw obscuring from the frame while on the tunnel (obscuring + double cover), or pre-sit it in a doorway so it flips to Engage and shoots with no move, beating Guard.
- **Teleport pads are Vantage, not killzone floor** — you can't be on the tunnel and on a pad at once, and big bases often can't emerge on them; the only use is base-touching a pad while on the tunnel to deny it.
- **Track Enemy scores better here** — elites voluntarily go on Guard, leaving more valid targets; block enemy banner spots with the tunnel and bodies.
- **Several hard matchups ease off in close quarters** — Canoptek (their matrix can't be measured through walls and they're less mobile), Murderwing (shorter charge threat), Plague Marines (tunnel-reinforce to gang up on isolated marines). But Plant Banner is oppressive in Into the Dark / Tomb World: opponents wall off and guard the banner, so keep the tunnel toward mid to contest.
- **On Descent (Tomb World two-board) maps, pick the level with two objectives** and place the tunnel on the descent markers so you can burrow then charge across to the other board.

## Generic Advice

- **"Don't get shot" is the discipline.** ~18 wounds and a 5+ with no defensive re-roll dies to two shots reliably — always ask "does this team have Saturate?" and avoid presenting a shot at all. Getting shot more than once on TP2 means you played too aggressively. [strong consensus]
- **Slow TP1–2, fast TP3–4.** TP1–2 are for banking CP, staging, and menacing the opponent; only move bugs to where the tunnel will be TP3. Accept ceding mid on TP2 and clap back — this single mindset shift wins games. [strong consensus]
- **Deploy all five above ground TP1, then burrow whoever should go under.** There's no downside — you disguise your plan, open doors/breach on close quarters, and keep flexibility. Stage within reposition+dash of where the tunnel will be. A common fake: stack bugs toward the enemy objective to bait an over-commit, then tunnel away. [strong consensus]
- **Hide in melee.** Charge an already-expended (ideally poisoned) enemy and you're immune to everything but a charge; you can parry "nothing" to keep hugging as a meat shield. Roughly half your charges should be defensive. Don't hug a model that can still activate, and don't try it vs shoot-into-melee / fight-first teams (Exaction, Battleclade). [strong consensus]
- **Chip to breakpoints.** Non-deniable damage — Felltalon bite, Burrowing Strike, poison, Death Frenzy — is how you finish things that out-fight you (an 8/10W body dies in 2 fights instead of 3). [strong consensus]
- **You only need 2 of 3 objectives to max crit — never plan around all three.** Go mid + the enemy home. You're a Crit-Op + Kill-Op team, not a TacOp team; count points every turn and go passive once you're ahead on crit/kill.
- **Set up at least one non-reciprocal (>7") charge each turning point** and stage so the enemy can hit you but you can't be hit back — force them to react (even seize) just to deny your kill.
- **You still must interact to score — you can't win purely passive, and once behind it's very hard to come back.** Trade up: before charging, weigh how many ready enemies can shoot you before you re-conceal, and whether the trade over-performs a 1-for-1.
- **Tempo tools for the option-heavy TP3–4:** use a chess clock and a fundamentals checklist (identify symmetric vs asymmetric threats, name the enemy's power pieces and threat ranges, set your tempo, activate symmetric → asymmetric → stall). Watch enemy and your own top-of-turn threat ranges and interrupts constantly.
- **Roll the Prime's CP most turns when he's safe** (he heals the self-damage) — but never when a bad result drops him past a breakpoint.
