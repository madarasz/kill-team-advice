# Murderwing - Advice

## TL;DR

- **Play cagey and evasive — you are near the bottom of marine survivability and cannot heal.** Counter-punch, don't initiate; stage in cover, strike, hide in combat, kill next turn. Playing MW like Deathwatch/AoD (charge the first thing you see) is how people lose. **[strong consensus]**
- **Retrieval is the default TacOp in almost every game** — the easiest 4-6 points on the board, and the whole reason the passive playstyle works. **[strong consensus]**
- **Auto-take core: Chaos Lord (dual/relic claws), Champion (power weapon + plasma pistol), Skysear (melta), Warp Talon, Shrieker.** Last 1-2 slots flex to Huntmaster / Depredator / Curseclaw / Raptors by matchup. **[strong consensus]**
- **The team is extremely map-dependent** — open maps (Volkus/Octarius) massively favor MW; CQ/Tomb World/ITD are the weak maps. Avoid terrain so your 6" boost charge plays like the old 8".
- **Post-nerf reality (as of this scrape):** the Aug '26 (Q3) BDS caps boost *charge* at 6" for healthy **and** injured ops alike (earlier logs citing 4" injured predate it — see Faction Rules); the two Apr '26 dataslates cut Malicious Narcissism, Warp Talon obscuring, Depredator's Carving Blow combos, and vox-cry range (3"→2"). Pure "retrieve and hide" no longer auto-wins — you must also stage aggressively and play the crit op.
- **Wings of Darkness is the standout ploy** (9" boost reposition); **Long Forgotten Honour is the signature save.** **Bladefins is a near-universal trap — don't take it.**
- **Razor wire is now near-mandatory** — flying MW ops ignore it, enemies don't, so it sets up the non-reciprocal charges the nerf otherwise took away.

## Operative Selection

Auto-takes first, tech/contested pieces last.

- **Chaos Lord — the one true auto-take.** Default is dual/relic lightning claws; the built-in **Ceaseless** is the key draw given the team's scarce rerolls. Second-best build is power weapon + bolt pistol (more consistent into weak-melee teams, where claws are "overkill"). Power fist is widely rated too inconsistent. Pair with clawed armour to hit kill breakpoints. **[strong consensus]**
- **Champion — near-must; power weapon + plasma pistol is standard.** He farms CP via his challenge, his power weapon out-damages most, and shooting still triggers the challenge. Power fist *only* to break 8-wound models. Lone dissenter (Sgarmark) tests dropping him; the room disagrees. **[strong consensus]**
- **Skysear — near-auto with melta; a flying move-dash-shoot melta is your tempo anchor** (~11-15" threat, strikes first even without initiative — your insurance in an elite meta). He also carries a bolt pistol. Plasma is a real option on open maps / into 8W ops for full range + guard flexibility; flamer is bad (only if magnetized). **[strong consensus on melta default]**
- **Warp Talon — near-auto even post-nerf; your best mid-board scoring piece.** Drop in (not an action → no guard triggered), retrieve or do a mission action, then reposition ~4" back to heavy cover. Note: *retrieving* does trigger guard; *dropping in to fight* does not. Post-nerf he lost innate obscuring and now costs 2 APL on arrival, so some value him a touch less. Run kraks alongside a dual-claw Lord.
- **Shrieker — auto-take scoring/tech piece.** Guaranteed stun + d3 mortals for visibility only, -1 APL aura on objectives, refuses enemy icon/mission actions (works even on Conceal). Classic play: sit on vantage doing the crit op, then Wings-of-Darkness ~9" on TP4 to steal the enemy home objective + retrieve = ~3-point swing. **[strong consensus]**
- **Huntmaster — the elite / anti-fall-back / power-weapon tech piece.** Chain snares stop fall-backs (pin an elite without fighting, then follow up with another operative for +1 to hit, making Lord/Warp Talon dice far more reliable). Good vs shooting hordes and JAS teams (Wreckas, BoK) since you want multiple crits. His flyby mortal (Strike/Slice from Above) is a niche bonus, not the reason to take him. Don't bring him into teams that *want* to be locked in melee (Exaction). **[consensus]**
- **Depredator — anti-horde / anti-soft-target tech, NOT an elite fighter.** Hits on 4s (5s injured) with no rerolls, but his spin/carving blow deals automatic mortals (min 2, ~4 avg) regardless of injury, one-shotting most 7-8W (even 13W) models. Reverb's heuristic: "anti-everything-softer-than-space-marines — does it have ~8 wounds? can you hit multiple at once? then he has value." Charge, spin (free mortals), then vox/counteract instead of fighting to pin safely. Divided: some (Sgarmark, Ben, Avid) refuse a 4s-hitting model and lean it out post-nerf; consensus keeps him as horde tech and benches him vs elites and vs Wreckas (JAS kills his normals). **[strong consensus on the role, genuine split on how often]**
- **Curseclaw — tech piece, not a staple, but rising in esteem.** Bring into elites at **13 wounds or less** — strike-on-death guarantees a trade (rending crits ≈ 14 wounds) and Snatch picks enemies up; avoid into 14W. Best vs hordes / low-wound / guard teams (snatch breaks guard by re-setting up the model). Snatch auto-succeeds on 8W-and-under. Big weakness: no rerolls ("if he had any rerolls he'd be an auto-include"). Several note the snatch tech is clunky and rarely lands, but JustJP/vers/Winged One increasingly rate him ("slept on"). **[warming consensus]**
- **Raptors — specialist pick, mainly vs Stealth Suits and the cagey mirror.** They ignore stun and APL/injury modifiers (fly full 6" while injured — though that last point is a disputed rules question, see Faction Rules) and a bare chainsword kills a suit. Take 1-4 into suits, 1-2 into other stun/movement-debuff or 10+ operative teams. "Oops all raptors" is a meme outside the suits matchup. **[consensus as a tech answer to stuns]**

**One-box priority build** (repeated near-verbatim across all chunks): Lord (claws), Champion (plasma + power weapon), Warp Talon, Melta Skysear, Shrieker, plus Huntmaster **or** Depredator. Magnetize Champ/Lord/Skysear arms. ~12 models covers most competitive options; 15 for full bolt-pistol variants. **[strong consensus]**

## TacOp Selection

- **Retrieval — the default and best-scoring TacOp.** 4 points easily, 5-6 with the Shrieker's late steal; Warp Talon makes it "very easy six points" (retrieve mid, fly to safety). Grab home + mid early, then force the opponent to overextend. Credited channel-wide as the crutch behind the team's ~48-50% win rate. **Post-nerf caveat:** pure retrieve-and-pass gets steamrolled by teams that out-activate — you must also stage aggressively / play the crit op. **[strong consensus]**
- **Dominate — roughly 50/50 with Retrieval, chosen by matchup read.** Take it when the opponent *must* rush into you (Goremongers, Fellgor, Mandrakes, Plague Marines) and on Volkus where the melta wins the board; it forces you onto Engage and to play the game. **[recurring]**
- **Flank — strong and underused, map/matchup dependent.** Especially good into 5-operative teams (5-6 points for little work — just don't forget to reveal it) and vs teams that won't cross the midline (Plague Marines, Deathwatch, Raveners). Warp Talon can teleport into breach-gated rooms first.
- **Sweep & Clear — mostly avoid.** Sitting on objectives on Engage invites shooting; regarded as a conservative 3-point floor at best, "when I need a loss" at worst.
- **Route — workable vs hordes on long-edge deployment (easy double-kills), but the 2-points-per-TP cap and post-nerf marine survivability make it harder to score.**
- **Scout Enemy Movement — experimental / fallback only.** Don't play it into hordes/Kroot (scored 1 point); theoretically OK into other uninteractive Volkus teams or to force heavy-weapon teams to move, but the Conceal requirement plays poorly for MW. Take Retrieval or Plant Devices over it.
- **Crit ops:** MW favors marker/mobile ones — Transmission, Stake Claim, Energy Cells, Loot, Secure — because it can appear 12" away as a late activation. **Retrieval does NOT mix with Energy Cells** (can't carry two markers, and cell-carriers can't grab retrieval tokens) — take Flank on those maps.

## Equipment Selection

- **Razor wire — near-mandatory.** Flying MW ops don't cross it, enemies do — so it sets up non-reciprocal charges (one of the only ways left post-nerf), controls objectives, and denies faster teams' reciprocal charges. **[strong consensus]**
- **Clawed Armour — staple, but only when the +1 charge damage crosses a kill breakpoint** (elves = 1 hit, most = 2, JAS/shield teams = 3). Must-take vs Deathwatch; now worth it vs Wolf Scouts (their DR is normals-only now); good into 8-9W and Raveners. Note: it only triggers on the 6" **boost** charge finishing in control range, and you can't use it *and* Depredator's spin in the same activation.
- **Kraks — a must whenever you run dual lightning claws** (a fall-back weapon / for non-melee teams); real payoff vs Wreckas/Stealth Suits/Aquilons/Salvagers. With only a Warp Talon, consider swapping kraks for smokes (drop in, tap point, smoke). **[strong consensus]**
- **Warp Fuel — excellent vs any fall-back team; the power is the *threat*** (a fall-back becomes a wasted activation, so it may never trigger). **Only works during the opponent's activation, not yours** (a repeatedly-corrected misconception). Useless vs reactive fall-backs like Stealth Suits' Retro-Thrusters.
- **Smoke grenades — near-mandatory post-nerf** for playing safer, hiding the Shrieker, surviving after a melee kill in the open, and the Murderous Descent setup. Strong into elves.
- **Vox-casters — an AOE stun to prevent fall-backs; value rose after the MN nerf.** Charge in, vox, and some teams can't fall back. **Note: the Apr '26 BDS cut the Vox-cry stun-test range 3"→2"**, so you must be closer to catch multiple targets. Pair with a Depredator spin so targets are >5W. Competes with stun grenades, not explosive grenades. Weak into shooty teams that just shoot you for it.
- **Bladefins — a trap; near-universally "pretend it doesn't exist."** 1 APL for d3+1, can't charge with it, and Huntmaster does the finish-a-fleeing-model job for free. **[strong consensus]**
- **Universal kit for safe staging:** light/heavy barricades (heavy gives the Warp Talon a 2+ obscured retreat spot), a Mine (strong vs Exodites), frag/stuns. The **Warp Talon + portable barricade** drop (2+ save, obscured, move the full 4") was strong pre-nerf, weaker now he lost innate obscuring.

## Faction Rule Advice

- **Boost is now a tactical positioning tool, not an offensive threat.** Use it to drop Warp Talon behind heavy cover, fly the Shrieker in for a steal, and cross razor wire that others can't. **[strong consensus]**
- **Injured boost charge is now 6", not 4" — but only since the Aug '26 (Q3) BDS.** The Apr '26 Emergency Update added "if it would BOOST during the Charge action, don't add the additional 2"" (so injured boost charge was **4"**); the **Aug '26 (Q3) BDS reworded this to "its move allowance cannot be higher than 6""** — so now healthy and injured both cap at 6". Earlier scrape chunks citing 4" injured are pre-Q3 and no longer correct. Running (non-boost) charge is unaffected at **8"** — so charge on foot when you need the reach.
- **Boost only happens during YOUR activation** — no boosting on Counteract, off Guard/Combat-Support interrupts, or during Murderous Descent's free charge. You *can* boost off LFH's fall-back if it's your activation. **[strong consensus, repeatedly re-confirmed]**
- **One boost action per activation** (can't stack two boost actions). Boost is a straight-line "teleport" — you don't exist in between (how you pass over walls/enemies), and you can't change direction mid-boost.
- **You cannot boost through doorways** (walk instead) — no 8" boost-charge through a door; on Volkus you boost through but ignore the Accessible tax, on Tomb World you can't boost through doors at all. Boosting still triggers the strategy ploys even on TW.
- **Clawed Armour needs an actual boost charge** finishing in control range — running in then charging does NOT proc it.
- **Snatch (Curseclaw):** the boost path must physically cross the target; start/finish outside any control range; contest roll only if base wounds ≥9 (add both wound stats; ≤8W auto-succeeds); place them anywhere in the boost zone behind their start, no farther from the Curseclaw than where they began (can end in your own control range, which ends the move). Uses: cancel Guard, drop enemies on mines/vantage/into your Lord's range, pull a model off a point, bypass shoot-into-combat.
- **Astartes chainsword strikes first — leverage it defensively** by sitting in a stronghold / on a home point so chargers eat your first strike.
- **Boons of Damnation (Chaos Lord):** one roll per action, beat current points to buff exactly ONE die (+damage) or negate 3+ damage to the Lord; procs on defense too; only boosts damage in melee. Keep the number **low** for reliability — racking to 6 (auto-use) is suicidal, "for the lolz" only. You can even suicide with points to deny Rout/Dominate. Vs first-damage-die teams (Goremongers) use the boon on the second die.
- **Raptors ignoring the injured movement penalty is an unresolved RAW question** (Daylovich argues it works; kid0krew/JustJP lean RAW-it-doesn't; TOs split). Treat as unsettled — ask your TO.

## Ploys

- **Wings of Darkness — the standout A/S-tier ploy.** 9" boost reposition (+3"), enables the Curseclaw ~9"+dash Snatch ("charging on Conceal"), late-turn Flank scoring, dropping snatched ops into your Lord's control range, and repositioning to safety after a fight/shoot. The key enabler of the whole toolkit. **[strong consensus]**
- **Long Forgotten Honour — the signature save.** Strike-on-death saves your melta/key models — but it needs crits (usually 2, or 1 on a fight), it ends the fight (all attack dice discarded), and you can only boost the fall-back in your own activation. Bait it with Huntmaster/Champion/Warp Talon to make crits easier; stage likely LFH ops up front when you lose initiative. Mostly useless vs pure shooting. FAQ bars the Warp Talon from it. **[strong consensus]**
- **Nightmare on High — a top strategy ploy vs shooting teams** (balanced defense, reroll all TP save CP and damage), great with a cover save. Needs the 6" charge to enable, so some rate it lower post-nerf.
- **Predators Above — best strat ploy, but only worth it when you'll land at least one (ideally two) boosts.** With 6" charges rarely triggering it, most now hold the CP as a command reroll (for the melta or Depredator). The defensive reroll-on-boost half is great easy value in full-send play. Can force rerolls onto AoD.
- **Malicious Narcissism (post-nerf) — guarantees 2nd-to-last activation at best; save it for the plasma gunner, to grab a crit point safely, or to punish an exposed op.** Strongest vs *horde* teams (freezes them up), weak vs elites (a normal pass is often better). The first MN of a TP sets the course.
- **Instil Fear — situational; applies to every enemy normal die of 3+ damage, but only while you fight (not retaliate), and only vs teams WITHOUT Lethal 5+, rending, or 3+ melee damage.** Best with 2-3 charges lined up. Doesn't reduce Damnation-roll damage.
- **Cull the Weak — niche, mostly vs melee/high-wound teams** — shoot stunned/wounded targets (esp. bolt pistols from vantage) to double-tap. Triggers only on the enemy's Fight action (not retaliation), checked at the *start* of your activation. Widely called overly conditional.
- **Murderous Descent — effectively a dead ploy.** Must be on Engage (can't flip from Conceal on an interrupt), needs heavy setup (barricades each side, 2" above, switching rooms). The one working line: smoke on your feet on an objective/vantage so the enemy either comes within 2" (→ you charge) or takes an obscured shot; can also bait a Stealth-Suit retro-thrust. Best on ITD to stop room entry. Most say "Descent never." **[strong consensus it's the worst ploy]**

## Generic Advice

- **Play cagey ~90% of the time — MW is near the bottom of marine survivability and can't heal, so counter-punch rather than initiate.** Play for TP3/4, grab objectives, retrieve on TP2 if safe; going aggro TP1-2 just loses you models. Treat it like Plague Marines / Nemesis Claw: let the opponent come to you. **[strong consensus]**
- **Don't kill on the charge vs hordes/mobile teams — stun + charge + hide-in-combat that TP, then kill next TP, repeat.** Keep your kill ratio high; never lose more than ~3 marines (the kill-op breakpoint). Pick charges carefully so you aren't killed after incapacitating one model.
- **Melee decision tree:** can I double-kill? → can I kill + tactical-hug? → else do nothing.
- **Pre-measure everything — 6" flying charges demand precision.** "If you can't set up 6" flying charges you're not measuring your opponent." Avoid terrain so a 6" boost charge effectively equals the old 8", and keep the full 8" *regular* charge for when you need reach.
- **Threaten a zone with the mere *option* to advance** — the opponent must respect your threat range even when you don't move (a mine by a window shuts down a sightline).
- **Build a terrain "maze" for non-reciprocal charges** — two light barricades with a gap + razor wire so the enemy must move into charge range while you don't; enforce the hazard with the Warp Talon. "Can't get within 2" to shoot, can't get within control range to charge → you win on retrieval."
- **Diversify your threats; don't stack two hammers on one op** (e.g. plasma + power fist) that becomes a huge loss when it dies. Put decision-making on the opponent.
- **Save CP for command rerolls and firefight ploys** over the situational strategy ploys — a CP-reroll melta doubling up is worth more than most strat ploys.
- **Double-stack -1 APL to keep a 2-APL model stunned** (prevents fall-backs even with +1 APL, survives single status-removal). MW is one of few teams that can output this many stuns.
- **On Volkus, stack a vantage to make charges impossible and shoot down with plasma** from 3rd-floor vantage late TP1/TP2 — but ask if the opponent has Seek Light before trusting vantage cover (bring smokes if they do).
- **Warp Talon token play:** deploy him as a pressure token; drop the retrieval token on Counteract so he acts as a counter-punch, or warp in to loot/retrieve mid then fly to safety. Slice-the-Veil materialize in the middle of a large room to solve staging headaches — it's not an action, so no enemy guard.
- **Tomb World is not as bad as its reputation — matchup skew matters more than map skew.** A favorable matchup on TW beats an unfavorable one on Volkus.
- **Master one team's ins-and-outs rather than counter-picking.**
